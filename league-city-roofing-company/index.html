Every League City re-roof is a windstorm job

League City sits almost entirely in Galveston County, and Galveston County is one of the Texas Department of Insurance's designated catastrophe areas. For a homeowner, that means one thing above all: a new roof has to be installed to the windstorm building code and inspected by a TDI-appointed inspector or engineer for the house to stay eligible for Texas Windstorm Insurance Association coverage. At the end you should get a WPI-8, the certificate of compliance.

Many League City homes carry their wind and hail coverage through TWIA, because private insurers have pulled back from the coast. If yours does, a roof without a WPI-8 can leave you unable to renew that coverage, and a future buyer unable to get it. It is the single most important question to ask a League City roofing company, and the answers sort them fast.

Questions that separate League City roofers

  • Do you handle the windstorm inspection and WPI-8? The answer should be yes, without hesitation, and the certificate should be in the contract as a deliverable.
  • When does the inspector come out? Some checks have to happen mid-job, while nailing and underlayment are still visible. Doing it all at the end is a warning sign.
  • What products, installed how? Windstorm-compliant roofs use rated shingles, underlayment and fasteners installed to the tested method. Get the product names in writing.
  • Can I have a copy of your insurance certificate? Texas has no state roofing license, so insurance and local references are your protection.

Small repairs count too. In the catastrophe area, repairs that touch the roof deck generally need the windstorm inspection as well. Ask before a patch job whether it needs one.

Why League City roofs age fast

This is the first line of the coast. Hurricane Ike in 2008 stripped roofs across Clear Lake and League City, Hurricane Harvey's rain in 2017 tested every flashing joint, and Hurricane Beryl's wind in July 2024 lifted shingles all over the county. Salt air works on metal flashing, drip edge and exposed nail heads. Many neighborhoods, from South Shore Harbour and Westover Park to Victory Lakes, Tuscan Lakes and Magnolia Creek, went up in the 1990s and 2000s and are now on their second roof or due for one.

City permit, HOA, then the windstorm inspection

There are three sets of eyes on a League City roof. The city's building department permits the work, most neighborhoods have an HOA that approves the shingle and color, and TDI's windstorm program certifies the installation. A roofer who works here every week will line all three up in the right order. Get the HOA approval first, so the permit and the inspection schedule do not stall waiting on it.

Claims and deductibles

Coastal policies often carry a named-storm or percentage deductible that is much larger than people expect. Texas law requires you to pay it, and it is illegal for a roofer to offer to waive or absorb it. You file the claim; the roofer can document the damage and meet the adjuster. Common questions

Does a new roof in League City need a windstorm inspection?

Yes, if you want the home to stay eligible for Texas Windstorm Insurance Association coverage. Galveston County is a designated catastrophe area, so a re-roof there has to be inspected by a TDI-appointed inspector or engineer and certified with a WPI-8.

What happens if my roof has no WPI-8?

The house may not qualify for TWIA wind and hail coverage, now or when you sell. Some owners can get an engineer to certify completed work after the fact, but it is harder and not guaranteed. Get it done during the job.

Are roofers licensed in Texas?

No. There is no state roofing license, so ask for a certificate of insurance, the city permit in the roofer's name and the WPI-8 as a written deliverable.

Which areas of League City do you take calls from?
