In The Woodlands, the covenants are the permit

The Woodlands is not a city. It is a township, and the roof over your house is governed less by a building department than by the deed covenants that came with your lot. The Woodlands Residential Development Standards require prior written approval for every roof replacement, and they say plainly that roofing and color changes need a permit at the time of repair or replacement, even where other minor repairs are exempt.

Approval comes from your village's Residential Design Review Committee or the Township's covenant staff. Each village has its own elected committee that meets monthly. A roofing company that works The Woodlands regularly knows this and builds the application into its timeline. One that does not can leave you with a finished roof and a compliance letter.

Before any shingle comes off: make sure the application is approved, in writing, for the exact material and color on your contract. Changing the color at the supply house after approval is how a new roof turns into a violation.

What the Township allows on a roof

The standards list composition shingles, tile, slate, stone-coated or standing-seam metal and cement-fiberboard as acceptable roofing, and they rule out glossy finishes on metal. The Township keeps a current chart of acceptable and unacceptable roofing materials, and anything not listed is decided case by case. Colors run to the earth tones that suit the forest. None of that stops you from choosing an impact-rated shingle: Texas Property Code section 202.011 bars an owners' association from prohibiting wind- or hail-resistant shingles that otherwise look like the allowed ones.

Roofs under a forest

The trees are the point of The Woodlands, and they are also what wears the roofs out. Shaded slopes never fully dry in Montgomery County humidity, so algae streaks and soft decking show up earlier than they would on an open lot. Pine straw packs into valleys and gutters and holds water against the flashing. And every tropical storm brings limbs down; Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 left trees on roofs all through the villages.

Age varies more here than almost anywhere around Houston. Grogan's Mill dates from the community's opening in 1974. Panther Creek, Cochran's Crossing and Indian Springs followed through the 1980s and 1990s, Alden Bridge and Sterling Ridge after that, and Creekside Park, across Spring Creek in Harris County, is the newest. A 1980s house in Panther Creek and a 2015 house in Creekside Park are completely different roof conversations, and a good roofer inspects before recommending anything.

Choosing among roofing companies in The Woodlands

  • They handle the covenant application. Material, color, and the approval letter in hand before work starts.
  • They look at the trees. Limbs over the roof, needle-packed valleys, and shaded slopes where the decking may be soft.
  • Proof of insurance, sent to you. Liability and workers' compensation. Texas does not license roofers, so this is your main protection.
  • A written scope. Tear-off, underlayment, ice-and-water shield in the valleys, drip edge, flashing and pipe boots replaced, a price per sheet for decking, and a workmanship warranty.
  • Honest about insurance. Texas law makes you pay your own deductible, and it is illegal for a roofer to offer to waive it.

Common questions

Do I need approval to replace my roof in The Woodlands?

Yes. The Woodlands Residential Development Standards require prior written approval for all roof replacements, through your village's Residential Design Review Committee or the Township's covenant staff. Get approval for the exact material and color before work starts.

What roofing materials are allowed in The Woodlands?

The standards accept composition shingles, tile, slate, stone-coated or standing-seam metal and cement-fiberboard, with no glossy finishes on metal. The Township keeps a current chart of acceptable materials, and other materials are considered case by case.

Can the covenants stop me from using impact-resistant shingles?

No. Texas Property Code section 202.011 bars an owners' association from prohibiting shingles designed to resist wind and hail, provided they resemble the shingles otherwise allowed.

Why do Woodlands roofs wear out early?

Shade. Slopes under the tree canopy stay damp, which feeds algae and softens decking, and pine needles hold water in the valleys. Falling limbs in storms add damage on top.

Which villages do you take calls from?

All of them: Grogan's Mill, Panther Creek, Cochran's Crossing, Indian Springs, Alden Bridge, Sterling Ridge, College Park and Creekside Park, plus nearby Shenandoah, Oak Ridge North and Spring.
