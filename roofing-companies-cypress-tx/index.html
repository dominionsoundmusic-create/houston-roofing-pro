Cypress has no city hall, so nobody inspects the roof

Cypress is not a city. It is a stretch of unincorporated northwest Harris County, run by the county, the school district and a patchwork of municipal utility districts. That has one big consequence for your roof: there is no city building department. Harris County's engineering office permits new homes, additions, structural repairs and anything in the floodplain, but an ordinary shingle replacement on an existing house does not go through it. On nearly every Cypress re-roof, no government inspector ever sees the work.

That makes the choice of roofing company, and the contract you sign with it, the whole of your protection. Your HOA will care about the color. Nobody else is checking the nails.

Write the inspection into the contract. Ask for photos of the bare deck before the underlayment goes down, photos of the flashing at walls and chimneys, and a price per sheet for any decking that has to be replaced.

Newer roofs: check the warranty before you call anyone

Cypress is still building. Bridgeland, Towne Lake, Cypress Creek Lakes and the subdivisions along the Grand Parkway and US-290 have thousands of homes less than ten years old. If yours is one of them and the roof is already lifting, leaking or shedding shingles, start with the paperwork, not a roofer:

  • The builder's warranty. Most new homes come with a written builder warranty for workmanship in the first year and longer coverage on some systems. Read yours before anyone else touches the roof.
  • The shingle manufacturer's warranty. Shingles carry a manufacturer warranty that often depends on how they were installed. A roof that fails young may be an installation problem the warranty covers.
  • Your insurer. If a storm did it, it is a claim, no matter how new the roof is.

A roofer who works Cypress regularly will ask how old the house is before quoting, and will tell you if the builder should be the first call.

Older Cypress, older roofs

The other half of Cypress is the Cy-Fair suburbs that went up through the 1980s and 1990s, such as Copperfield, Coles Crossing and the neighborhoods off FM 1960 and Jones Road. Many of those houses are on their second roof or due for a third. On a roof that has been replaced before, ask whether the old layers will be torn off to the deck. Laying new shingles over old ones hides whatever is wrong with the decking, and the next roofer will have to take both off.

Wind, hail and the Cypress claim

Northwest Harris County gets the spring hail that tracks across the Katy prairie and the summer and tropical wind that lifts shingle tabs. Hurricane Beryl in July 2024 left lifted and missing shingles across Cypress. After a storm, the out-of-town crews arrive fast, often knocking on every door on the street. Take your time. File the claim yourself, let the adjuster see the roof, and remember that Texas law requires you to pay your own deductible. A roofer who offers to cover it is breaking the law. Common questions

Do I need a permit to replace my roof in Cypress, TX?

Generally no. Cypress is unincorporated Harris County, which has no city building department. The county engineering office permits new construction, additions, structural repairs and floodplain work, not an ordinary shingle replacement. Your HOA may still need to approve the shingle.

My house is only a few years old and the roof is failing. Who do I call?

Start with the builder's warranty and the shingle manufacturer's warranty, then your insurer if a storm caused it. A young roof that fails early may be an installation problem someone else should pay for.

Should new shingles go over my old ones?

Usually not. A full tear-off to the deck lets rotten decking be found and replaced, and a second layer only makes the next replacement harder and costlier.

Are roofers licensed in Texas?

No. Texas has no state roofing license. Ask for a certificate of insurance and addresses of recent Cypress jobs.

Which parts of Cypress do you take calls from?

All of it: Bridgeland, Towne Lake, Cypress Creek Lakes, Fairfield, Coles Crossing, Copperfield, Blackhorse Ranch, and the neighborhoods along US-290, FM 1960, Barker Cypress, Jones Road and the Grand Parkway.
