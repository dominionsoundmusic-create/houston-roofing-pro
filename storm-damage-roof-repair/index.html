Houston storm season is long

Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30, and Houston's spring and summer thunderstorms fill in the rest of the calendar. The last few years have shown how much damage wind alone can do here. The May 2024 derecho hit downtown with hurricane-force gusts and no hurricane in sight. Hurricane Beryl came ashore that July as a Category 1 storm and still stripped roofs and dropped trees across the metro. Most storm damage to Houston roofs is wind and falling trees, often followed by rain getting into the opening.

The first hours after the storm

  • Safety first. Stay clear of downed lines and report them to CenterPoint and 911. Do not go into rooms with sagging, wet ceilings.
  • Stay off the roof, and off ladders in the wind and rain.
  • Photograph everything before anything is moved: the roof from the ground, fallen limbs, debris, and every stain and wet spot inside.
  • Prevent more damage. Policies generally expect reasonable steps like an emergency tarp. Keep every receipt. See emergency roof repair.
  • Dry things out fast. In Houston heat and humidity, wet drywall and insulation start growing mold within a day or two.

Wind damage and flood damage are different claims

This catches many Houston homeowners. A standard homeowners policy covers wind: shingles torn off, a tree through the roof, rain that came in through that opening. It does not cover rising floodwater, which needs a separate flood policy, usually through the National Flood Insurance Program. New flood policies normally have a 30-day waiting period, so buying one when a storm is already in the Gulf is too late.

Coastal homes: in Galveston County and other coastal areas, wind coverage may come from the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association rather than your homeowners policy, with its own claim process and its own building-code certification for repairs. Check which policy covers wind before you file.

Filing the claim

You file the claim with your insurer; the roofer's job is to document the damage and, ideally, be on the roof when the adjuster comes. Read your policy for the wind and hail deductible, which in Texas is often a percentage of the home's insured value, and for whether the roof is paid at replacement cost or actual cash value. Hail has its own page: hail insurance claims.

After the storm, the storm chasers

Every big Houston storm brings crews from out of state knocking on doors. Take your time. Ask for proof of insurance, a local address and references, get the scope in writing, and do not pay most of the price upfront. Under Texas law, a contractor may not pay, waive or rebate your deductible.

Common questions

Does homeowners insurance cover hurricane damage to my roof?

Wind damage from a hurricane is usually covered by homeowners insurance, or by a windstorm policy on the coast, subject to the deductible. Flooding from storm surge or rising water is not; that requires separate flood insurance.

Should I tarp my roof after a storm?

If the roof is open, yes, and it is best done by a roofer. Keep the receipt, since reasonable emergency repairs are usually part of the claim.

How soon should I report storm damage?

As soon as it is safe. Policies expect prompt notice, and roofers' schedules fill quickly after a major Houston storm.

What is the difference between wind and flood damage?

Wind damage comes from above: torn shingles, fallen trees, rain through an opening. Flood damage comes from rising water. They are covered by different policies.
