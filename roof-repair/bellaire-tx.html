Roof Repair in Bellaire, TX

Bellaire has 1950s ranch houses standing next to 2020 rebuilds on the same block, under oaks that predate both. That mix means no two roofing jobs here are quite alike, and the low-slope sections catch everybody out.

The low-slope section is where the leak is

Most Bellaire houses of any age have at least one section that is nearly flat — a porch roof, a garage, a rear addition, a carport. On a pitched roof gravity does most of the waterproofing. On a low slope, gravity does almost nothing, and the roof has to be genuinely watertight rather than water-shedding.

Which means it needs a different system entirely: a membrane, torch-down modified bitumen, or a single-ply product. Shingles on a low slope will leak, no matter how carefully they are laid, because water sits rather than runs.

If your leak is over a porch, a garage or a flat-roofed addition and a contractor proposes shingles, ask what slope they have measured. Below about 2:12 shingles are not an appropriate material and most manufacturers void the warranty.

This is also why a house can have a perfectly sound main roof and a chronic leak in one room for years.

Neither is better. But a contractor quoting an old Bellaire ranch from a photograph is guessing, and the guess is usually low. Ask for the decking and layer count to be verified rather than assumed, and for a per-sheet decking price in writing.

Mature oaks, and what they do here

Bellaire's tree canopy is one of the reasons people live here, and it is genuinely good for a roof — shade slows ultraviolet ageing meaningfully.

The costs are specific. Leaf litter in valleys and gutters, which dams water. Limb contact, which abrades shingles in ordinary wind. And limb falls, which are the real risk — a mature oak limb carries far more weight than a pine branch and can crack decking outright.

Keep six to ten feet of clearance where you can, and have the roof checked after any significant limb fall rather than waiting for a leak.

When a neighbour rebuilds

A teardown next door affects your roof in ways worth knowing about. Construction debris lands on it. Mature trees usually come out, changing how much sun you take. And a new structure two storeys taller than the old one alters wind flow across your roofline, which can lift edges that were fine for decades.

None of it is catastrophic. It is worth an inspection after the work finishes, and worth photographing your roof before it starts.

Before you call

  • Whether the leak is over a flat or nearly flat section — that changes everything.
  • Roughly when the house was built and whether it has been added to.
  • Whether you know how many roof layers are on it.
  • Whether trees overhang, and whether a limb has come down recently.
