Roof Repair in Humble, TX

Humble sits under heavy pine and has flood history in parts, and both leave marks on a roof. Neither is dramatic. Both are the reason roofs here need attention that roofs a few miles west do not.

Pine debris is the main enemy here

Loblolly pine drops needles all year rather than seasonally, and they mat into a felt in valleys and gutters that water cannot pass. The mechanics of what that does to a roof are set out on the Spring page and apply identically in Humble.

The Humble-specific point is frequency: under the canopy in the older neighbourhoods, three clearances a year is realistic rather than the usual two, and one of them should follow the heaviest drop.

What flooding leaves behind

Parts of Humble have flood history, and water on the ground never touches a roof. What the remediation afterwards does to an attic is a different matter, and it is covered on the Friendswood page.

The short version for Humble: if your house flooded and had attic work, say so when you call. It changes where a roofer looks first.

Limb strikes look like nothing

A branch comes down, you clear it off, the roof looks fine. That is the dangerous case.

A limb strike can fracture the shingle mat and crack decking beneath while leaving the surface almost unmarked. The shingle still lies flat, but its seal is broken and the substrate is damaged.

It shows as a leak six or twelve months later, by which point linking it to the storm for a claim is difficult. Photograph the limb before you move it, note the date, and have the roof checked within a week.

Before you call

  • Whether the leak is near a valley — the first question under pine canopy.
  • How long since valleys and gutters were cleared.
  • Whether a limb has come down recently.
  • Whether the property flooded and had attic work afterwards.
