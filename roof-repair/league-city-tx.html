Exposure decides what fails first

Wind does not press down on a roof, it lifts. Air accelerating over the surface creates uplift concentrated at the ridge, rakes, eaves and corners — and how much of it reaches your roof depends almost entirely on what stands between you and open sky.

On a mature street with tall trees, the canopy breaks the wind before it arrives. On an open lot in a newer subdivision with waist-high landscaping, it arrives at full strength.

Practical consequence: two identical houses of the same age with the same shingles can be years apart in condition purely because one has trees and the other does not. If your ridge caps have lifted and your neighbour's have not, exposure is usually the reason rather than a defect.

Salt, at a distance

League City is close enough to the bay that salt reaches exposed metal, though less aggressively than the communities directly on the water. It shows up the same way, just slower: flashing corroding from the back, vent bases rusting at the seams, and fasteners losing grip.

Worth including in an inspection rather than assuming the shingles tell the whole story. On a roof at year twelve, the metal may need attention while the field has years left.

Clear Creek and the flood question

Parts of League City have flood history, and water on the ground does not damage a roof. What follows can. Homes that took water often had attics opened repeatedly for remediation, insulation removed, and equipment moved through a space never designed for traffic.

Disturbed flashing and damaged decking from that work surface years later as a leak nobody connects to the flood. If your house flooded and had attic work done, say so — it changes where a roofer looks first.

Before storm season

  • Have ridge and edges checked in spring, not when a storm is named.
  • Photograph the roof from the ground, all four sides.
  • Clear limbs back from the roofline where trees overhang.
  • Check whether your deductible is a percentage rather than a flat figure — near the coast it usually is.

Before you call

  • Whether your lot is open or sheltered by mature trees.
  • Roughly when the house was built and whether the roof is original.
  • Whether the problem is at an edge or ridge, or in the middle of a slope.
  • Whether the property has flooded and had attic work.
