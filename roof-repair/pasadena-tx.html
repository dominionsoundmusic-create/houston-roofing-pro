Roof Repair in Pasadena, TX

Pasadena built most of its housing for the refineries in the post-war decades, and those roofs have been patched by whoever was available for sixty years. What is under the shingles matters here more than what is on top.

Sixty years of patches

A house built in 1955 has had a great many people on its roof, and not all of them were roofers. The result is a layer of history that a contractor has to work through before anything else.

  • Roofing cement over flashing, often several generations of it. It is a legitimate emergency measure and a poor permanent fix — it hardens, cracks, and then traps water rather than shedding it.
  • Vents sealed over rather than replaced, which blocks ventilation the roof depends on.
  • Mismatched patches where a slope was partly redone at some point.
  • Nails through the field of the roof from a tarp that was fastened wrong after some storm.

This matters when you get a quote. On a house this age, an honest contractor spends the first part of the visit working out what previous people did, and that assessment is worth paying for. One who quotes from the driveway has not done it.

The decking decides everything

On an older Pasadena roof, the question is not whether the shingles are worn. It is whether the wood underneath can still hold a nail.

Decades of small leaks soften decking slowly. A repair fastened into soft decking does not hold, and neither does a new roof. That is the single fact that determines whether a repair is worth doing.

Check this, from inside the atticWhat it means
Dark staining on the underside of the deckingWater has been getting in, possibly for years
Spongy feel when pressedThe wood has lost integrity. It needs replacing, not covering
Daylight anywhere other than a ventA breach. Find it before it rains
Sagging between raftersDecking has absorbed moisture and deformed
Plank rather than plywoodCommon on this vintage. Gaps between boards mean less for fasteners to grip

Go up on a dry day with a torch. You do not need to be an expert to notice any of those, and knowing before a contractor arrives changes the conversation considerably.

Repair or replace, on an old roof

Repair is right when the decking is sound, the problem is localised, and the rest of the roof has life left. A cracked pipe boot or a failed section of flashing on a serviceable roof is a straightforward job.

Replacement is right when decking needs significant work, there are multiple leaks in different places, or the roof is already at two layers with more failures coming. At that point repairs are money spent on something being replaced within a year or two anyway.

Before you call

  • Roughly when the house was built.
  • What you found in the attic, if you looked.
  • Whether there are multiple leaks or one.
  • Whether you know how many times it has been roofed.
