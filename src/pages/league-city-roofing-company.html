League City roofing runs on two sets of rules at once

League City roofing has a feature most Houston suburbs do not: a re-roof here answers to the city's Building Department and to the state's windstorm program at the same time. Most of League City lies in Galveston County, one of the 14 first-tier coastal counties that the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association lists as its designated catastrophe area. A smaller part of the city is in Harris County, which is outside that area, so check which county your address is in.

That means a roofing job in League City produces more paperwork than the same job in Katy or Cypress, and the paperwork is what protects you later. The city's own building ordinance ties the two systems together: the permit holder has to hand over a copy of the Texas Department of Insurance report certifying that the roof meets the state building specifications. Skip that step and you can end up with a roof that looks fine but leaves gaps in your file when you renew wind coverage or sell.

The paperwork trail on a League City re-roof

The order matters. Each step below comes from League City's building rules or the state windstorm program, and each one leaves a document you should keep.

  1. Neighborhood approval, if you have an association. Many League City subdivisions have deed restrictions that cover roof color and material. Get the approval letter first so the permit and the inspection schedule do not stall waiting for it. Texas Property Code section 202.011 bars an association from prohibiting impact resistant or energy efficient shingles that look like the shingles it already allows, so ask about those if you want them.
  2. The city permit. League City's ordinance requires a permit from the Building Official before a residential roof inside city limits is replaced. Applications are filed on the city's online permit portal. The roofer should file in its own name and show you the permit number.
  3. Windstorm inspection during the job. In the catastrophe area, roof work has to meet the windstorm building code and be inspected while it is under way, when the decking, underlayment and nailing can still be seen. Ask who schedules that inspection and on which day.
  4. The TDI report and the WPI-8. When the work passes, the Texas Department of Insurance issues a certificate of compliance, form WPI-8. League City's ordinance says the contractor or owner responsible for the permit must provide a copy of the TDI report certifying compliance.
  5. A closed permit in your file. The city points out that proof of a permit is often needed for insurance and when you resell the home. Put the permit, the WPI-8, the contract and the shingle warranty registration in one folder.

Repair or re-roof: where League City draws the line

League City's list of permits issued by the Building Department puts the threshold at 100 square feet, one square in roofing terms. Replacing more shingles than that counts as re-roofing and needs a permit. A small repair such as a new pipe boot, a few shingles torn loose by a summer squall or a short run of loose flashing usually sits under it.

That threshold is worth knowing when a roofer proposes a big "repair". If the work covers several squares, it is a permitted job whatever the estimate calls it, and in the catastrophe area it also falls under the windstorm rules. Ask the roofer to write the square footage on the estimate so you can see which side of the line the job sits on.

The city also restricts one material. Its building regulations say wood shingles and shakes are not allowed on new residential structures, and repairs to an existing wood roof are allowed when they cover less than 25 percent of the roof area. If an older house still carries wood, ask what the city allows before you price a replacement.

Storms that reached League City roofs

League City has lived with Gulf weather since the days the town was called Clear Creek. Galveston businessman J. C. League gave the town his name in 1893, and the State of Texas incorporated League City on May 14, 1962, according to the city. Hurricane Harvey in August 2017 was mostly a flood event here: Community Impact reported that about a quarter of the city's homes flooded. Flood water and roof damage are separate claims, usually under separate policies, so keep the two sets of paperwork apart.

Hurricane Beryl came ashore near Matagorda on July 8, 2024 as a Category 1 storm. Its winds crossed the whole region, and Community Impact's Bay Area edition reported more than 2.2 million CenterPoint Energy customers without power as it made landfall. Less than two months earlier, on May 16, 2024, the derecho that the National Weather Service office in League City documented pushed winds over 100 mph through parts of the Houston metro. A roof that rode out both storms can still have shingles whose seal strips let go without anyone noticing, and the first sign is often a ceiling stain in the next tropical downpour.

If your roof has not been looked at since 2024, that is a reasonable time to call. Many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call, and ask for photos of anything the roofer finds.

Why League City homeowners call the line

The windstorm step is easy to miss

An out-of-area crew may not plan for an inspection in the middle of the job. When you are put through, ask straight away whether the company schedules windstorm inspections and delivers the WPI-8, and listen to how it answers.

Wind and hail coverage questions come first

Galveston County addresses, which covers most of League City, are inside the area where the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association writes wind and hail coverage. Whoever insures yours, call the insurer before work starts on storm damage, and keep the roofer's photos for the adjuster.

One number at any hour

Rain coming through a ceiling does not wait for business hours. Calls are picked up around the clock and transferred, and if the roofer is busy your name, number, city and need go to it so it can ring you.

No fee from you

Calling is free. Any estimate comes in writing from the roofing company itself, and you settle the bill with that company directly, or with claim money from your insurer. A company may pay the line a referral fee.

Questions to ask a League City roofer before you sign

  • Will you pull the League City permit in your company's name, and when will I see the permit number?
  • Who schedules the windstorm inspection, on what day of the job, and when will I receive the WPI-8?
  • Which shingle, underlayment and fasteners will you use, and are they the products the windstorm code calls for here?
  • How many square feet does this job cover, and does that put it over the city's 100 square foot permit line?
  • What is your price per sheet if the decking is rotten, written into the contract?
  • Can you send me your certificate of liability insurance, and the addresses of a few roofs you finished nearby?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover, for how long, in writing?
