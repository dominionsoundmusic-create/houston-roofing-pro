A county seat that is smaller than its neighbors

Roofing in Richmond, TX depends on which Richmond you mean. The city is the Fort Bend County seat, a town of 11,627 people at the 2020 census. The mailing address covers far more: communities such as Pecan Grove, which counted 22,782 residents that year and sits outside any city limits.

The town itself is one of the oldest in the region. The city's history says Richmond was laid out in early 1837 by Robert Eden Handy and William Lusk, near a fort at a bend of the Brazos that gave the county its name, and it was among the first towns the Republic of Texas incorporated. Around that old core, newer subdivisions have filled the farmland along the Grand Parkway and FM 359 over the last few decades.

Who permits a roof with a Richmond address

Three different authorities can have a say, depending on where the house sits. Your property tax statement lists the taxing units on your lot; if the City of Richmond is not one of them, you are on county land.

  • Inside the cityThe City of Richmond Building Department takes permit applications through the city's online permit portal. Its permit office is at 600 Morton Street, 281-232-6871, weekdays 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.
  • Contractor registrationThe city amended its building code to require contractors working in Richmond to register, and registration is needed to pull a permit. Its notice said the process had no fee.
  • Unincorporated Fort Bend CountyCounty engineering requires a residential development permit for new structures or additions of 400 square feet or more. A plain re-roof is not on its list; ask at Permits@fbctx.gov or 281-633-7500 if unsure.
  • Your associationMost master-planned communities need architectural approval for a new roof color or material, and that holds whether or not any government permit applies.

The city's contractor registration notice dates from several years back, so confirm today's requirements with the permit office before the roofer starts. If a roofer working inside the city cannot show a Richmond registration, it cannot pull the permit itself, and that is worth knowing before you sign. Fort Bend County is inland of the Texas Department of Insurance's designated catastrophe area, so no TDI windstorm inspection applies to a Richmond roof.

The Brazos, Harvey and Beryl: what Richmond roofs have been through

The Brazos River runs right past downtown, and the weather service keeps a river gauge at Richmond. The river is a flood threat more than a roof threat, but the storms that raise it also soak every roof in town for days. These events come from the National Weather Service's river gauge record and from Fort Bend County figures reported by Community Impact.

If any of these storms left a stain on a ceiling that was painted over rather than traced, ask the roofer to start in the attic. Many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call.

Older Richmond, newer Richmond

Pecan Grove shows how fast the area around the city has grown. The census counted 13,551 people there in 2000 and 22,782 in 2020. Its houses span several decades of building, so a single street can mix original roofs, second roofs and recent storm replacements. Big trees are part of the appeal and part of the roofing problem: shade keeps leaves and moisture on shingles, and a heavy limb is the most common way a tropical storm punches a hole in a roof.

Farther out, communities like Harvest Green, where home sales began in 2015 according to a 2019 Houstonia feature, are young enough that many roofs are still the builder's originals. There, the first question after a leak is whether the builder's written warranty or the shingle maker's warranty applies, or whether a storm caused it and it belongs with your insurer.

The HOA step comes before the order

Associations in these communities usually review roof colors and materials. Texas Property Code section 202.011 says an association may not forbid shingles designed to resist wind and hail, or to cut energy use, so long as they resemble shingles it already allows. Send the committee the product name and color and wait for the approval letter before the roofer orders material.

Why Richmond homeowners call the line

Not sure which rules apply

City or county, permit or not, registration or not. A roofing company that takes Richmond work every week should sort that out before it writes a price, and should name the office it will deal with in the contract.

A limb on the roof after a storm

The oaks and pecans that shade older Richmond streets are the first thing tropical wind brings down. Keep everyone away from the limb and any wire it touched, report downed lines to CenterPoint Energy, and let a roofer judge the damage.

A roof older than the paperwork

Many owners in Richmond's established neighborhoods do not know how many layers are on the roof or when it last came off. A careful roofer checks the edge and the attic and tells you what it found before quoting a tear-off.

One number for any hour

Storms do not keep office hours. The line answers around the clock, and if the roofing company it reaches cannot answer, it is handed your name, number and need so it can ring you back, and you are not dialing down a list in the dark.

What to ask a Richmond roofer before you sign

Rules and records

  • Is my address inside the City of Richmond, and if so, are you registered with the city?
  • Which permit will you pull, from which office, and will it be in your name?
  • Will you prepare my association's architectural submittal?
  • Can your insurer send me a certificate of liability insurance directly?

The roof

  • How many layers are on it now, and will you remove all of them?
  • What is your written price per sheet if decking is soft from old leaks?
  • Will you look in the attic for stains from past storms before you quote?
  • What written workmanship warranty do you give, and will you register the shingle warranty?
