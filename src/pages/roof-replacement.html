When a Houston roof is ready to be replaced

Roof replacement in Houston is a bigger decision than a repair, and the age printed on a shingle wrapper does not settle it. Sun, heat and storm after storm wear a Gulf Coast roof unevenly, so one house on a street can need a new roof years before the house next door.

What decides it is the condition of the shingles, the deck and the flashing, and how often water is getting in. This page walks through the signs that a roof is finished, what a replacement actually involves, the permit question, the materials homeowners here usually choose, and what the day of the tear-off is like.

Houston Expert Roofers does not replace roofs. Think of it as a switchboard that costs you nothing: an automated assistant picks up day or night, asks about the roof and your part of town, and transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofing company. That company inspects the roof, writes the quote, pulls any permit and handles the tear-off and install, and your payment goes to that company.

Signs the roof is finished, not just damaged

A single leak points to a repair. The signs below point to a roof that is wearing out everywhere at once. Most can be seen from the yard or from inside the house, so there is no reason to climb up and look.

Granules piling up

The colored grit on an asphalt shingle shields the asphalt from sunlight. A little washes off a new roof. A steady layer in the gutters or at the downspouts years later means the shingles are losing their protection and aging faster.

Curled or cupped edges

When the edges of shingles lift or the middles sink, the shingle has dried out and no longer lies flat against the course below. A curled shingle catches wind and lets rain run underneath it.

Leaks in more than one room

One stain is usually one failed part. New stains over the bedroom, the hall and the garage in the same season suggest a roof that is failing in several places, which is where patching stops paying off.

A soft or sagging deck

If the roofline dips between rafters, or the roofer reports soft decking across a wide area, the wood has taken on water over time. That is replaced during a tear-off, not covered over.

Daylight in the attic

Light showing through the deck anywhere other than a vent opening means there is a gap water can use too. Note it from the attic hatch and tell the roofer when you call.

Widespread storm damage

Hail bruising or wind creasing across several slopes rarely patches cleanly, and new shingles seldom match weathered ones. See hail damage and storm damage for how that is judged.

If a roofer recommends a whole new roof because of one leak, ask which of these signs it found and ask for photos. A second opinion is reasonable on a job this size. The roof repair page explains when a targeted fix is the better answer, and the roof lifespan guide covers how long different materials tend to hold up here.

Two quotes for the same house can look far apart simply because one leaves things out. Ask every roofer you talk to for a written, itemized quote covering each line below, so you are comparing the same job.

Permits for a new roof in Houston and the suburbs

Inside the City of Houston, the Houston Permitting Center issues a residential roofing permit for any re-roof or overlay on a home. Its page says the permit involves plan review and an inspection, stays valid for six months, and must be kept on site until the final inspection is approved. If the job changes the framing that holds the roof up, plans have to be submitted as well. Only roof covering work of 100 square feet or less in total is exempt. Kingwood is inside the city, so the same rule applies there.

The city adopted the 2021 International Residential Code, with local amendments, for work from January 2024, according to the International Code Council. Among other things, that code does not allow a new roof covering to be laid over a roof that already has two or more layers. After the May 2024 derecho, Houston Public Works expedited re-roof and overlay permits and reminded owners that the work must also follow their neighborhood's deed restrictions.

Suburban cities such as Katy, Sugar Land, Pearland, League City and Conroe have their own building departments and their own rules, and homes in unincorporated Harris, Fort Bend or Montgomery County are handled differently again. Near the coast, in the Texas Department of Insurance catastrophe area, a new roof also has to pass windstorm inspections.

Materials Houston homeowners usually weigh

The roofer you are connected with will recommend a material for your house and budget. These are the common choices, described as general product information, not as an endorsement of any brand.

Architectural asphalt shingles

The most common choice on Houston houses. They are thicker and more dimensional than the older flat three-tab style. Each shingle has a strip of sealant that the sun heats until it bonds to the course below, which is why manufacturers say shingles installed in cold weather may take longer to seal.

Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles

Roof coverings tested under the UL 2218 standard are rated Class 1 to 4; for Class 4, a two-inch steel ball is dropped from 20 feet onto the same spot twice without cracking the product. The Texas Department of Insurance says a Class 4 roof qualifies for the largest premium credit, but each insurer sets its own discount and paperwork, so ask your insurer before choosing. Texas Property Code 202.011 limits an HOA's power to ban impact-resistant shingles that resemble the ones it allows.

Metal

Standing seam and other metal panels shed water quickly and suit Houston's heat, and some panels carry an impact rating too. Metal needs a roofer that installs it regularly, since the trim and fasteners are where it fails. See the metal roofing page.

Tile

Clay and concrete tile appear on some Mediterranean and Spanish style homes around Houston. Tile is heavy, so changing to it from shingles can mean checking the framing, which inside the city brings in the plan submission mentioned above.

Under the shingles, ask what underlayment is going on. It comes as traditional asphalt-saturated felt or as synthetic sheets, and the industry wrote a separate ASTM standard (D8257) for synthetic products in 2021. The product name should be in the quote.

Two warranties, two different promises

The manufacturer's warranty

This comes from the company that made the shingles and covers defects in the product. It does not cover mistakes in how the roof was installed, and many have a period of full coverage followed by a prorated period. Some require registration after the job. Ask the roofer to register it and send you the confirmation.

The roofer's workmanship warranty

This is the roofing company's own promise to fix problems caused by its installation, such as a flashing detail that leaks. It is only as good as the company behind it, so get the length and what it covers in writing, and ask how long the company has worked under its current name.

A shingle warranty with a big number of years does not help if the leak was caused by the install, and a workmanship warranty does not help if the company is gone. You want both in writing.

What install day is like

  1. Before the crew arrives. Move cars out of the driveway and garage approach, take fragile items off walls that share the roof framing, and bring patio furniture and grills away from the house. A dumpster or trailer is often parked in the driveway.
  2. Tear-off. The old shingles and underlayment come off, usually a section at a time so the house is not left open. This is the loudest part of the job.
  3. Deck check. With the deck exposed, the roofer replaces any soft or rotten sheets at the per-sheet price in the quote and should tell you how many it replaced.
  4. Dry-in and install. Drip edge, underlayment, valley lining, flashing and boots go on, then the shingles and ridge caps or vents.
  5. Cleanup and inspection. Ask how the crew will clean up, including running a magnet over the yard and driveway for loose nails. Inside the City of Houston, the permitted job then gets its city inspection. Keep the permit, invoice, photos and warranty papers together.

How long it takes depends on the size and pitch of the roof, the material and the weather, so ask the roofer for its estimate and what happens if rain moves in midway.

Start times, weekends and noise

People often ask what time roofers start work and whether they work on weekends. It varies from one roofing company to the next. OSHA's guidance to roofing employers recommends scheduling strenuous work for cooler parts of the day, which is one reason crews here tend to start early. Inside the City of Houston, the noise ordinance exempts construction sound only during daytime hours and only up to a set level measured at the neighbor's property line. Deed restrictions and HOA rules can be stricter about hours and weekend work. Ask the roofer for its start time and work days in writing, and warn your neighbors.

Timing around storm season

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 through November 30. Right after a hail or wind event, many roofers are booked solid and door-to-door crews appear. If your roof is worn but holding, scheduling the replacement away from those rushes gives you time to compare quotes and check each company.

Questions to ask before you sign

  • Which signs of wear did you find, and can I see your photos of the roof and the attic?
  • Is this a full tear-off, and how many layers are coming off?
  • What is your price per sheet of decking, and will you show me what you replaced?
  • Which shingle, underlayment and vents are you using, by product name?
  • Who pulls the permit, and which office issues it for my address?
  • Will you register the manufacturer's warranty, and what does your own workmanship warranty cover?
  • Can you send your certificate of liability insurance before work starts?
  • What time does the crew start, which days do you work, and where will the dumpster go?
