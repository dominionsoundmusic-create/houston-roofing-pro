A bayfront city with its own wind standard

Roofing in Seabrook, TX is shaped by the water. The city sits on Galveston Bay and Clear Lake in Harris County, and it has written its exposure into its building rules: the City of Seabrook says every structure must meet its adopted wind design requirement of 150 mph, measured as an ultimate design wind speed.

Two more things set Seabrook apart. The city's list of work that needs a permit includes roof repairs as well as new roofs, so a patch job here is not automatically permit free the way it can be in some suburbs. And State Highway 146 runs through town as a line on the state windstorm map: the part of Seabrook east of 146 is in the Texas Department of Insurance's designated catastrophe area, and the part west of it is not.

Seabrook permits, step by step

  1. Permit first, for repairs too. The city's permit list includes roof repairs and new roofs, and it says permitted work cannot begin until the Building Department issues the permit.
  2. Watch the fee warning. Seabrook's building permit application says starting work without a permit may lead to the permit fees being doubled. Roof replacement is listed among the residential renovation and repair categories on that form.
  3. Build to 150 mph. Ask the roofer to name the products and fastening it will use to meet the city's adopted wind design requirement, and to put them in the contract.
  4. East of 146, plan the windstorm inspection. The inspection happens while the decking, underlayment and nailing are still visible, so it has to be scheduled during the job, not after.
  5. Keep the file. The permit, the WPI-8 where it applies, the contract and the shingle or panel warranty. Seabrook's public information request log shows owners asking the city for exactly these records when insurers or buyers want them.

The Department of Community Development handles building permits at 1700 First Street, and its office number is 281-291-5669. Rules and fees change, so confirm the current process with the city.

After a storm: what Seabrook did after Harvey

When Hurricane Harvey damaged homes in 2017, the City of Seabrook set up a Storm Damage Recovery Permit. Its written procedure let owners list the repair work with approximate costs, and allowed them to keep removing damaged material from the home before applying for or receiving that permit. Whether the city opens a similar process after a future storm is its decision, so watch the city's announcements.

Seabrook roofs have had more recent tests. Hurricane Beryl came ashore near Matagorda on July 8, 2024 and, according to Community Impact's Bay Area edition, left over 2.2 million CenterPoint Energy customers without power across the region. Wind off open water drives rain sideways under shingle edges and around flashing, so a roof that came through with no visible damage can still have a weak spot. Many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call.
