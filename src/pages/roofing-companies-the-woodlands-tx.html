A planned forest with a design review instead of a building department

Hiring a roofing company in The Woodlands, TX works differently than it does in most suburbs. The Woodlands is governed by a township, and for a house roof the rules that matter come from the deed covenants on your lot. The Residential Development Standards treat a roof replacement as an exterior change that needs approval before the old shingles come off.

The second difference is the forest. The community was laid out to keep as many trees as possible, and the result is houses tucked under tall pines and oaks with greenbelts behind the back fence. That canopy is why people live here. It also drops limbs in storms, keeps north slopes damp and fills valleys with needles. A roofer who works these streets thinks about both the paperwork and the trees before it writes an estimate.

Getting a new roof approved in The Woodlands, step by step

The Township's Covenant Administration department runs the process. This is the order most homeowners follow, based on the Township's own permitting pages and its 2024 Residential Development Standards. Confirm the details for your lot with Covenant Administration at 281-210-3800, because village and neighborhood criteria can add requirements.

  1. Pick the material and the exact color first. The standards accept tile, slate, composition shingles, stone coated or standing seam metal and cement fiberboard products. Colors are to be muted, without a pattern, and in harmony with the natural landscape. The application should name the product line and color, not just "dark shingles."
  2. Register on the Civic Access Portal. The Township takes residential permit applications online only. If your roofer applies on your behalf, you still need your own account before the application can be completed, and fees are paid by card or e-check.
  3. Submit and answer questions. Staff or your village's Residential Design Review Committee reviews the request. Samples may be requested for roofing, and staff contact you if anything is missing. Approval arrives by email.
  4. Start work only on the approved product. If the supply house is out of the approved color, stop and ask before substituting. A change of color after approval is a change that needs its own approval.
  5. Keep the approval with the house records. When you sell, a buyer's agent may ask whether exterior changes were permitted. The approval email answers that question in one line.

The Township revised its roofing standards on May 21, 2025, after its Development Standards Committee took up rules on roofs, roof color and materials. Older summaries online may not reflect that revision, so ask for the current document.

What the standards say about roof materials

The chart below summarizes the roof material language in the 2024 Residential Development Standards. It is a reading aid, not a ruling: anything unusual is decided on application, and the current roofing standards control.

MaterialListed as acceptable?What to watch
Composition (asphalt) shinglesYesMuted color with no pattern. Impact rated versions are protected by Property Code 202.011 if they look like allowed shingles.
Standing seam metalYesNo glossy finish of any kind. Pick a matte or low sheen color and name it on the application.
Stone coated metalYesSame finish and color rules as other metal.
Tile and slateYesHeavy: ask the roofer whether the framing was built for that weight before switching from shingles.
Cement fiberboard productsYesColor must still read as muted against the trees.
Anything not on the listDecided on applicationExpect samples and a longer review.

Vents, flashing and other roof accessories are expected to match or coordinate with the roof color, which is worth mentioning to the roofer before it orders materials. A bright galvanized pipe jack on a dark roof is the kind of detail a review notices.

Why Woodlands homeowners call the line

Beryl put trees on houses

On July 8, 2024, the day Hurricane Beryl crossed the Houston area, The Woodlands Fire Department answered 235 calls between 5 a.m. and midnight, including 35 trees on homes, 32 trees blocking roads and six downed power lines, Community Impact reported. In Grogan's Mill a tree fell through a roof and trapped two people inside. A limb strike can crack decking well beyond the hole you can see.

The derecho came first

Beryl arrived while the Township was still hauling debris from the May 2024 derecho. At the July 18, 2024 board meeting, chief operating officer Chris Nunes called it a "double hit," and the board approved $2 million for Beryl debris removal. Roofs that came through May with loosened shingles took a second round of wind eight weeks later.

Shade that never dries

Slopes under a closed canopy stay wet long after a rain, which feeds algae and moss and keeps moisture in the decking around valleys and at roof to wall joints. Pine needles pack into those valleys and into gutters, so water backs up under the shingle edges. Leaks here are often slow and show up first in the attic.

Hail that comes and goes

Hail is not the main story in The Woodlands, but it does fall. National Weather Service storm data for April 2000 lists 1.75 inch hail in The Woodlands, and KPRC reported quarter size hail from a thunderstorm in June 2025, based on NWS storm reports. Small hail rarely ruins a roof on its own, but it can bruise older shingles and dent soft metal vents.

Village by village: roof ages differ by decades

The Woodlands grew one village at a time, so a roofer's first question is often which village you live in. Grogan's Mill, the original village, opened on October 19, 1974, according to Community Impact. Panther Creek followed in 1977, then Cochran's Crossing and Indian Springs, then Alden Bridge, Sterling Ridge and College Park as the community spread north and west. Creekside Park, across Spring Creek on the Harris County side, is the newest and is in the last stages of build out.

That spread matters. A house in Grogan's Mill or Panther Creek may be on its third or fourth roof and may have original framing details, older plywood and skylights that a new roof has to work around. A house in Creekside Park may still be on its first roof, and the question there is usually storm damage or a builder detail that never sealed well. A good inspection looks at the age of the deck and the ventilation, not only the shingles.

The Township has also lost a large share of trees. The Houston Chronicle reported that Beryl alone destroyed about 860 trees, citing Township documents, and that the Township has started replanting after losing about 7,000 trees to Beryl, bark beetles and other causes. Fewer trees can mean more sun and wind on roofs that used to be sheltered.

Questions to ask a roofing company in The Woodlands

About the approval and the trees

  • Will you prepare the covenant application, or do I file it myself?
  • Which product line and color will you list, and will you order only that?
  • Which limbs over the roof should be cut back before the new roof goes on?
  • Will vents, pipe jacks and flashing be painted or ordered to match the roof color?

About the work and the paperwork

  • What did you find in the attic: deck condition, ventilation, signs of old leaks?
  • What is the price per sheet if the decking under the shade side is soft?
  • How will you clean the valleys and protect them from pine needles going forward?
  • Can you send your certificate of liability insurance and a written workmanship warranty?
