Why more Houston homeowners are asking about metal

Metal roofing in Houston used to mean barns, sheds and rural shops. It now shows up on farmhouse-style homes in new subdivisions, on renovated bungalows and on houses rebuilt after storms. The appeal is easy to see: long panels with few laps, systems that carry published wind uplift ratings, and finishes that can reflect a lot of Gulf Coast sun.

It is still a big decision. The price, the way metal behaves in hail and the way some insurance policies treat it all differ from a shingle roof. This page sets out the choices, the questions to ask and the rules that apply, so you can talk to a roofer with your eyes open.

Metal roof examples: the main types you will be offered

Most quotes in greater Houston come down to two families of panel, plus a few specialty products. The difference that matters most is how the panel is fastened.

Panel makers' own comparisons describe exposed-fastener panels as the lower-cost choice and note that their screws must be checked and sometimes tightened over the life of the roof, while standing seam clips stay hidden under the seams.

Gauge, metal and coating

Steel thickness is sold by gauge, and a lower number means thicker metal. Residential steel panels are commonly 22, 24, 26 or 29 gauge. Angi's guide to gauges suggests 24 gauge where hail is common, describes 26 gauge as the most common choice for homes because it balances durability and cost, and suggests 29 gauge for sheds and outbuildings where looks matter less. Steel panels also carry a metallic coating under the paint, most often galvanized (zinc) or Galvalume (an aluminum and zinc alloy), and the two hold up differently around salt. If you live near Galveston Bay or Clear Lake, ask the roofer what the panel maker's warranty says about distance from salt water before you choose a metal.

Heat, wind and noise in a Houston summer

Heat and cool finishes

Roof temperature depends mostly on reflectance and emittance. ENERGY STAR cites Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory: a clean white roof reflecting 80 percent of sunlight stays about 50 degrees Fahrenheit cooler on a summer afternoon than a gray roof reflecting 20 percent. Berkeley Lab also tested cool pigments on metal panels: a cool brown reflected about 27 percent of sunlight, against about 8 percent for a conventional brown.

Wind uplift ratings

Metal panels are rated by uplift testing. For a TDI product evaluation, formed metal panels must be tested to UL 1897, UL 580, ASTM E1592 or FM 4474, and the published evaluations list how much uplift the panel resists at each clip or fastener spacing. Closer spacing usually means a higher rating, so the spacing on your roof should match the rating you are relying on.

Rain noise

The loud metal roof most people remember is on a barn with no deck and no insulation. Manufacturers note that panels over a solid deck, underlayment and an insulated attic sound much like a shingle roof inside the house. Ask what deck and underlayment the roofer plans to use.

Hail, cosmetic damage and your insurance

Hail can dent a metal roof without letting water in. That gap between how a roof looks and how it works is what insurance policies argue over. The Texas Department of Insurance lists four points for homeowners considering metal: a premium discount is not offered by every insurer and varies when it is; metal can still be dented by hail, debris and branches; a discount may come with conditions such as an exclusion for cosmetic damage; and coverage terms can change at renewal.

The idea goes back a long way. A 1998 TDI bulletin approved an optional homeowners endorsement that excludes cosmetic hail damage on impact-resistant roof coverings that earn a premium credit, defines cosmetic damage as a change in appearance that does not let water through or stop the roof from doing its job, and makes the endorsement void unless the policyholder signs it. The Texas Windstorm Insurance Association's manual also includes a cosmetic damage exclusion endorsement (TWIA 420) for residential policies, covering cosmetic hail damage to roof coverings.

None of this means metal is a poor choice. It means you should read the policy before you buy the roof:

  • Does my insurer give a discount for this roof, and how much?
  • Would a cosmetic damage exclusion be added, and how does the policy define it?
  • Is the panel rated for impact resistance under UL 2218, and does that matter to you?
  • What could change at renewal?

HOAs, deed restrictions and permits

Many subdivisions around Houston have deed restrictions and an architectural review committee that approves roof materials and colors. Texas Property Code section 202.011 limits what a property owners' association can do, but its wording is about shingles: an association may not bar an owner who is allowed to install shingles from choosing shingles designed to resist wind and hail, to improve heating and cooling efficiency or to generate solar power, as long as they resemble the shingles allowed in the subdivision, are at least as durable and match the look of the surrounding property. A switch to standing seam panels is not clearly covered by that protection, so read your restrictions and get the committee's approval in writing before you sign a contract. Metal shingle and tile profiles exist partly for this reason.

Inside the City of Houston, a re-roof needs a residential roofing permit from the Houston Permitting Center; roof covering work up to 100 square feet in total is exempt. Suburban cities and the coastal windstorm area have their own rules, covered on each town page.

Barns, shops and outbuildings on acreage

Out toward Magnolia, Tomball, Waller County and the rural edges of Fort Bend, plenty of properties have a shop, barn or carport under exposed-fastener panels. Those roofs need different care from a house: fasteners that have backed out or lost their washers, rust where panels overlap, trim lifted by wind and ridge caps that have worked loose. If a barn roof is leaking or rattling, mention it on the call and ask the roofing company you are connected with whether it works on outbuildings.

Questions to ask a metal roofing company

About the panel and the install

  • Standing seam or exposed fastener, and why for my house?
  • What gauge, what metallic coating and what paint finish?
  • Which uplift test or TDI product evaluation covers this panel, and at what clip or screw spacing will you install it?
  • Will you tear off the old roof, and what deck and underlayment go under the metal?
  • How will you flash walls, chimneys, valleys and pipe penetrations?

About paperwork and warranties

  • Can you send your certificate of liability insurance?
  • Will you pull the permit, and do I need HOA approval first?
  • What does the panel maker's paint and metal warranty cover near salt water?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover, in writing?
  • What maintenance will this roof need, and how often?
