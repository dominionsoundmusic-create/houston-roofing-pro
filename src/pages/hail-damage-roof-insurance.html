The honest answer to "how much will insurance pay?"

How much insurance pays for a roof replacement in Texas is set by your policy: the insurer's estimate of covered storm damage, minus your deductible, minus depreciation if the roof is covered at actual cash value. Two neighbors with identical hail damage can get very different checks.

The three things that decide it are already written in your policy before the storm arrives: the kind of deductible you have, whether the roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value, and any exclusion or endorsement that applies to roofs. This guide explains each one, the order of a claim, the deadlines, and what Texas law lets a roofer do and not do.

Nobody can promise you a payout, and this page does not. Houston Expert Roofers is only a phone line, free to call, that connects you with an independent local roofing company. It does not file, handle or negotiate claims, and it gives no insurance advice. For questions about your own coverage, ask your insurer or agent, or call the Texas Department of Insurance consumer help line at 800-252-3439.

How a roof payout is figured

The Texas Department of Insurance describes two ways a homeowners policy can pay for a roof. Replacement cost coverage pays up to the full cost to repair or replace it at today's prices. Actual cash value coverage pays that cost minus depreciation, an amount taken off for the roof's age and wear. On top of either, your deductible is subtracted from the claim.

Replacement cost: two checks

With replacement cost coverage, TDI says the insurer usually pays in two parts. The first check is the estimated repair cost minus depreciation and minus your deductible. The rest, often called recoverable depreciation, is paid after the insurer receives the bill for the finished work. Policies usually set a time limit for completing the repairs, so ask your insurer for that date in writing and keep the final invoice.

Actual cash value: one check, and the gap is yours

With actual cash value, the depreciation is never paid back. The older the roof, the bigger the deduction, and on an old roof the payment after the deductible can be small or nothing at all. The difference between that payment and the roofer's price is yours to cover.

Your own figures will differ, and the insurer may revise its estimate when it receives the roofer's invoice or a supplement for damage found during tear-off. If you have a mortgage, TDI notes the check is usually made out to you and your mortgage company. Ask your lender early how it releases claim money for repairs.

Deductibles, older roofs and cosmetic damage

Percentage deductibles

Many Texas policies carry a separate wind and hail deductible set as a percentage of the home's insured value. TDI's own example: a 5% deductible on a home insured for $150,000 is $7,500, so a $6,500 roof repair would be paid entirely by the homeowner. Turn the percentage into dollars before you file. A deductible applies to each claim.

Older roofs and roof schedules

TDI warns that as roofs age, some insurers switch roof coverage to actual cash value at renewal, and a roof in poor condition may not be covered at all. Look for a roof payment schedule or an actual cash value roof endorsement in your policy. Texas Windstorm Insurance Association policies use endorsement TWIA-804, which covers the dwelling at replacement cost and the roof covering at actual cash value.

Cosmetic damage exclusions

Some policies exclude cosmetic hail damage: dents or marks that change how the roof looks but not how it sheds water. TDI has long allowed such an exclusion on policies that get a premium credit for an impact-resistant roof. Ask your insurer directly whether your policy has a cosmetic damage exclusion and how it defines cosmetic.

If you are shopping for a new policy, TDI's advice is to ask how the roof is covered before you buy, not after a storm. If you install impact-resistant shingles, ask your insurer what discount it offers and what paperwork it needs; TDI says each company sets its own.

Filing a roof claim, step by step

  1. Stay safe first. Keep off the roof and away from downed lines. Indoors, catch drips and move belongings out of the way.
  2. Document from the ground and inside. Photograph dented gutters, downspouts, the air conditioning unit, shingles in the yard, ceiling stains and wet insulation you can see without climbing. Note the date and time of the storm.
  3. Prevent further damage, but do not rebuild yet. Your policy expects reasonable steps to stop more damage, such as a roofer's temporary tarp. TDI warns that an insurer might deny a claim if permanent repairs are made before it sees the damage, so keep receipts and wait on the permanent work.
  4. Report the claim promptly. Phone the insurer or use its app, get a claim number and write down every name and date. Read your policy for its deadline. TWIA wind and hail claims must be filed within one year of the damage.
  5. Meet the adjuster. Be there for the inspection. Your roofer may attend and talk through its estimate and what it found; it may not negotiate the claim for you.
  6. Read the insurer's estimate line by line. Check that it includes tear-off, decking where needed, underlayment, flashing, drip edge, ridge vent, disposal and permit. Ask about anything missing. Once the insurer has everything it requested, TDI says it has 15 business days to decide whether to pay.
  7. Hire, finish and close out. Sign with the roofer you choose, pay your deductible, and send the final invoice to the insurer to request any recoverable depreciation.

What a roofer may and may not do under Texas law

Texas has specific rules for contractors working on insured storm damage. The Texas Department of Insurance sums them up in its "Roofing and insurance: know the law" guidance. The short version is that the roofer works on the roof and the claim stays yours.

A roofer mayA roofer may not
Inspect the roof and give you a written estimateOffer to waive, rebate or absorb your deductible (Business and Commerce Code 27.02)
Discuss its estimate and other technical information with your insurer or adjusterAct as your public adjuster, or advertise to adjust claims on a property where it provides or may provide contracting services (Insurance Code 4102.163)
Submit its own invoice or a supplement for damage found during the workLeave out the deductible notice: a contract of $1,000 or more paid with insurance proceeds must state that you are responsible for paying the deductible
Set up a payment plan with you for your shareInflate an invoice or hide a discount so the insurer pays your deductible for you

Under Insurance Code chapter 707, you must pay your deductible, and your insurer may ask you for proof. TDI lists the kinds of proof that work: a canceled check, a money order receipt, a credit card statement, or a copy of a payment plan with your contractor. TDI says a contractor that offers to cover a deductible faces a fine of up to $2,000 and up to six months in jail, and it asks people to report such offers to the Texas Attorney General's Consumer Protection Hotline at 800-621-0508.

If the claim is denied or the payment looks low

Ask for the reason in writing

A letter that says only "no covered damage" does not tell you what to fix. Ask which policy wording the insurer relied on, and request a copy of the adjuster's estimate and photos. Compare them with your roofer's estimate line by line.

Send what was missing

If the insurer questioned the storm date, send dated photos and news or weather reports from that day. If it missed a slope or a line item, ask for a second inspection and say exactly what to look at.

Read the appraisal section

Many homeowners policies include an appraisal provision for disputes over the amount of a loss, where each side picks an appraiser. Your policy says whether you have one, how to start it and who pays for it.

Get qualified help

A public adjuster licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance can represent you for a fee; your roofer cannot. An attorney can advise on a dispute. TDI takes complaints about insurers at 800-252-3439.

Can I get my roof replaced for free?

This is one of the most searched roof insurance questions, and the honest answer is no, not through a legitimate claim. Even when a policy covers a full replacement at replacement cost, the deductible is yours, and Texas made it a crime for a contractor to absorb it. On an actual cash value roof, you also pay the depreciation gap. Door-to-door pitches for a "free roof" after a hailstorm usually rely on a promise the law forbids, or on signing over control of the claim to someone who cannot legally act as your adjuster.

What a claim can do is bring the cost of storm damage down to what your policy says you owe. Knowing that number before you sign anything is the best protection you have.
