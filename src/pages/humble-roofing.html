An oil town with a big mailing address

Roofing in Humble, TX means different paperwork depending on which Humble you live in. The city is small and old. The Humble mailing address wraps around it and reaches far more households than the city itself, most of them outside its limits.

The town took its name from Pleasant S. Humble, who ran a ferry on the San Jacinto River in the 1800s, according to the Handbook of Texas. Oil turned the crossroads into a boomtown: the Humble field, just northeast of town, was the largest producing oil field in Texas by 1905, and the Humble Oil and Refining Company, which carried the town's name, later became part of Exxon. The city incorporated in 1933.

That history is still visible in the housing. Streets near the old townsite hold the oldest houses, while newer subdivisions spread out toward Lake Houston and the river. An old house and a new one call for different conversations with a roofer, and the section below explains why.

Sorting out a Humble address before you sign anything

Who issues the permit, who you call about a downed line and which rules the roofer has to follow all depend on the jurisdiction, not the envelope. Work through it in this order.

  1. Check your tax statement. Your property tax bill or appraisal district record lists each taxing unit on your land. If it names the City of Humble, you are inside the city.
  2. Inside the City of Humble. The city issues building permits and takes applications through a permits portal on its website. Ask the roofer whether your re-roof needs a city permit, and if it does, have the company pull it in its own name and show it to you before tear-off. Do not rely on a fee you read somewhere; ask the city for the current one.
  3. In unincorporated Harris County. Much of the area around Humble, including Atascocita, has no city government. There is no city roofing permit and no city inspector. Harris County's Office of the County Engineer issues permits in unincorporated areas, mostly for development and floodplain work, so ask it if you are unsure. On a county re-roof, the written contract and the roofer's photos are your record.
  4. Across the river in Kingwood. Kingwood is part of the City of Houston, which requires a residential roofing permit for a re-roof or overlay. If your house is there, read the Kingwood page.

A roofing company that works the Humble area should ask which of these applies in the first conversation. If one says no permit is ever needed anywhere around Humble, it does not know the area.

Older Humble houses: what a roofer may find under the shingles

A house near the original townsite has usually been re-roofed more than once. Each time, someone decided whether to tear off the old layer or lay new shingles over it, and whether to replace tired decking. You inherit those decisions. These are the things worth asking about before you accept a price.

A second layer

Shingles laid over old shingles add weight, hide rot and shorten the life of the new roof, because the top layer cannot lie flat. Ask whether your roof has more than one layer and price a full tear-off to the deck.

Plank decking

Many older homes were built with board decking rather than plywood sheets. Gaps between boards can be too wide for modern shingle nailing. A roofer may recommend new sheathing over the planks; ask why and what it costs per sheet.

Little or no attic ventilation

Older attics often have a couple of small vents and nothing else. In a Gulf Coast summer that bakes shingles from below. A replacement is the natural time to add ridge and soffit vents, while the deck is open, so ask what the roofer plans.

Newer homes in the subdivisions around Humble have different weak points: complex rooflines with several valleys, pipe boots and flashing that age faster than the shingles, and builder-grade materials reaching the end of their life all at once on the same street. Harvey, the West Fork and Humble roofs

Humble sits beside the West Fork of the San Jacinto River, upstream of Lake Houston. In Hurricane Harvey the National Weather Service gauge on the West Fork near Humble crested at 69.18 feet on August 29, 2017, its highest on record, beating the 66.39 feet set in October 1994. A City of Houston report on the emergency dredging that followed counts more than 16,000 homes and 3,300 businesses damaged near Lake Houston.

Floods are not roof events, but they leave roof questions behind. Houses that took water were gutted and rebuilt in a hurry, and the attic was rarely the priority. If your house flooded, ask the roofer to look at the attic for old water stains, mold on the underside of the decking and any ceiling joists that were cut or patched during the rebuild.

Two more recent storms hit the whole region. The National Weather Service office in League City documented the May 16, 2024 derecho, and Hurricane Beryl on July 8, 2024 knocked out power to more than two million CenterPoint Energy customers. Both lifted shingles and dropped limbs across greater Houston, so a roof in Humble that has not been looked at since then is worth checking if you see a new stain.

Why Humble homeowners call the line

A leak on a house that has been patched before

On an older roof with years of repairs, a new drip can come from any of them. Callers want a roofer who will trace the water in the attic rather than add another dab of roofing cement on top of the last one.

A whole street of roofs the same age

When a subdivision is built in a few years, its roofs wear out together. Once a few neighbors replace theirs, other owners start asking whether theirs is next. A roofer with photos from your own roof answers that better than the neighbors can.

Pines and pine straw

Humble is wooded, and pine needles pile up in valleys and gutters and hold water against the flashing. Shaded slopes grow dark algae streaks. Neither is an emergency, but both shorten a roof's life if nobody deals with them.

Not knowing whose rules apply

City of Humble, unincorporated county or City of Houston: the answer changes the paperwork. Callers want a company that already works on their side of the line and does not have to guess.

Before you hire a Humble roofer, ask

  • Is my house inside the City of Humble, and does this job need a city permit? Who pulls it?
  • How many layers of shingles are on the roof now, and is your price for a full tear-off?
  • Is my decking plank or plywood, and what do you charge per sheet if some has to be replaced?
  • What ventilation will the new roof have at the ridge and soffits?
  • Can you send a certificate of liability insurance with my name on it?
  • What is your workmanship warranty, in writing, and how do I register the shingle maker's warranty?
  • If this is an insurance job, will your contract carry the deductible notice Texas law requires?

Any insurance claim is filed by you, with your insurer. The roofer can document damage, but under Texas law it may not act as your public adjuster on a house it works on.
