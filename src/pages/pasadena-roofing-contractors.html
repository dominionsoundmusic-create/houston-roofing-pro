Pasadena roofing contractors work under two sets of rules

Hiring among Pasadena roofing contractors starts with one map question: is your house east or west of State Highway 146? Most of the city lies west of it and follows ordinary city permit rules. The slice east of the highway is part of the state's coastal windstorm area, and a new roof there has to pass inspections the rest of Pasadena never sees.

Both halves share the same weather. Pasadena sits in southeast Harris County, south of the Houston Ship Channel, and much of it drains to Vince Bayou, Little Vince Bayou or Armand Bayou, all of them Harris County Flood Control District watersheds. Gulf storms, summer squall lines and the occasional derecho reach it with little to slow them down.

East or west of State Highway 146: what changes for your roof

In a Commissioner's Order dated December 1996, the Texas Department of Insurance designated the parts of Pasadena and Shoreacres east of State Highway 146 as catastrophe areas. A TDI bulletin says the designation took effect March 1, 1997. TDI's Harris County windstorm page still lists Pasadena with La Porte, Morgan's Point, Seabrook and Shoreacres.

The City of Pasadena roofing permit, in plain terms

Pasadena's posted fee schedule sets out three rules that matter to anyone hiring a roofer:

  • The 25 percent trigger. Replacing or repairing more than 25 percent of each roof needs a permit. A full tear-off always crosses that line; a handful of replaced shingles usually does not.
  • A flat residential fee. The schedule lists one residential roofing permit fee per structure, covering an overlay or replacement with like materials plus minor decking and structural work approved by the building inspector or plan reviewer. Confirm the current amount with the city, since fee schedules change.
  • No new wood shakes. Wood shingles, shakes and other combustible roofing are prohibited in any roofing project, except minor repairs to existing single-family homes.

The Permit Department works from City Hall at 1149 Ellsworth, on weekday business hours, and the city says no permits are issued after 4:30 p.m. Its permit line is 713-475-5575. Commercial roofs follow a separate fee, and the city asks for engineered drawings when a commercial roof is altered or structurally changed, which matters to owners of shops and offices as well as homes.

Storms that shaped Pasadena roofs

The last decade gave Pasadena roofs a hard run. Each storm below is documented by a public agency or by local reporting, and each left the kind of damage a roofer looks for years later.

  1. Hurricane Harvey, August 2017. Harvey is remembered for rain more than wind. The Harris County Flood Control District counts roughly 120,000 flooded structures across the region, and Pasadena's bayous were part of that. For roofs, days of rain found every cracked boot and loose flashing joint.
  2. The derecho, May 16, 2024. The National Weather Service office in League City describes a swath of winds over 100 mph across the Houston metro. Lines of storms like that lift shingle tabs across whole neighborhoods at once.
  3. Hurricane Beryl, July 8, 2024. Beryl reached the Texas coast as a Category 1 storm and knocked out power to more than two million CenterPoint Energy customers. Torn shingles, fallen limbs and bent gutters were common across east Harris County.

A shingle whose seal broke in 2024 can lie flat and look normal from the street, then let wind-driven rain underneath in the next squall. If your roof has not been looked at since Beryl, and you see granules piling at the downspouts or a new ceiling stain, it is worth a call. The highway line is easy to get wrong

A contractor who mostly works west of SH 146 may not plan for a windstorm inspection. When you call, say which side of the highway you are on, and ask the company you reach how it schedules the inspection and delivers the WPI-8.

Questions to put to Pasadena roofing companies

Rules and paperwork

  • Is my address east or west of SH 146, and how did you check?
  • Will you pull the city permit, and when will I see the permit number?

The roof itself
