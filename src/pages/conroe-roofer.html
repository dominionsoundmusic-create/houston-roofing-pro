A sawmill town that became one of the fastest growing cities in the country

Roofing in Conroe, TX covers everything from a century-old frame house near the courthouse square to a roof that is two years old in a subdivision that did not exist a decade ago.

The town began in 1881, when Houston lumberman Isaac Conroe built a sawmill on Stewarts Creek, according to the Texas State Historical Association. Rail lines met here, and by 1889 Conroe had taken the Montgomery County seat. Timber built it, and the pines that fed those mills still shade a great many Conroe roofs.

Growth came fast in this century. A 2017 Census Bureau release named Conroe the fastest growing large city in the nation between 2015 and 2016, at 7.8 percent. The bureau's QuickFacts put the July 2024 estimate at 114,581 people, more than a quarter above the 2020 base of 89,973. That means thousands of young roofs on new houses, beside older roofs in established areas that are due for their second or third replacement.

When a Conroe roof needs a city permit

The City of Conroe's permit FAQ draws the line in one sentence: re-roofing a house does not require a permit providing the structural sheathing or roof rafters do not need to be replaced. In practice, the deck decides.

  1. Before the contract. The roofer checks the attic and the roof surface for soft decking, sagging between rafters and water stains on the wood. Ask what it saw and for photos.
  2. Shingles only. If the sheathing and rafters are sound, a tear-off and new shingles inside city limits needs no city permit, and no city inspector comes out.
  3. Rot found mid-job. If boards have to be cut out and replaced once the old shingles are off, the job has crossed the city's line. The roofer should contact the Building Inspections and Permits Department (permits@cityofconroe.org, 700 Metcalf St.) before closing that roof up.
  4. Apply online. Since December 8, 2025, the city takes building permits and inspection requests through its OpenGov portal.
  5. Outside city limits. Much of the Conroe mailing address is unincorporated Montgomery County, whose permitting FAQ says residential roof replacement needs no permit. Commercial roofs can need a fire code permit.

Put the decking rule into the contract: a price per sheet for replaced decking, a promise to photograph the bare deck, and a sentence saying who contacts the city if structural wood comes out. A roofer that already works in Conroe will expect the question.

Storms on the Conroe record

Conroe sits well inland, so hurricanes arrive weaker than they do on the coast. Hail, thunderstorm wind and falling trees do most of the roof damage here.

The KHOU story carries a lesson worth repeating: the family learned after the tree fell that the policy had lapsed. Before storm season, confirm your homeowners policy is active and find your wind and hail deductible on the declarations page. If you have questions about your coverage, ask your insurer; the insurance claims guide lists what to ask.

After a storm in Conroe, stay off the roof. A wet roof is slick, and decking under a fallen limb can give way. Most Conroe homes get electricity from Entergy Texas (your bill names your provider), so report a downed line to the utility or call 911, and stay well back from it. Inside, move belongings out from under the leak, catch the drip with a bucket or towels, and keep everyone out of a room with a sagging ceiling. Take photos from the yard and from inside, then call your insurer and a roofer.

Why Conroe homeowners call the line

A new roof that already has a problem

In a city growing this fast, a lot of Conroe roofs are only a few years old. A leak at a pipe boot or a missing ridge cap on a newer house raises its own questions: is it still under the builder's or the shingle maker's warranty, and who should fix it? A roofer can tell you what failed so you know whom to call.

Pine straw and shade

Needles pack into valleys and gutters and hold water against the shingles. Shaded slopes stay damp and streak with algae. Many Conroe callers want a roofer to find out whether a slow leak starts in a clogged valley rather than in the field of the roof.

A lake house in the open wind

Lake Conroe has held water since 1973, behind a dam built by the San Jacinto River Authority with the City of Houston, and its shoreline is lined with homes. Houses facing the lake have no tree line on the water side. Shingle edges, ridge caps and metal trim there take the full force of a passing storm. Owners want someone who looks hard at the windward edges and the fastening, not just the field.

An older house near downtown

Frame houses near the square and in the older neighborhoods around it may have plank decking, several shingle layers or rafters that have seen a century of humidity. That is exactly where the city's permit line can come into play, and callers want a roofer that will tell them before tear-off.

Shingles, metal and the HOA in Conroe

Asphalt shingles cover most Conroe houses. On a replacement, three choices carry the most weight in this climate: an underlayment suited to wind-driven rain, enough ventilation to keep the attic from baking in August, and whether to step up to an impact-resistant shingle after a hail history like this one. Some insurers treat impact-resistant roofs differently, so ask your own insurer before you decide. The roof replacement cost guide explains what drives the price.

Metal turns up on lake houses, shops and rural property around Conroe. Standing seam and exposed-fastener panels are very different products, and the metal roofing page explains how.

Many of Conroe's newer subdivisions have deed restrictions that require approval of roof color and material. Texas Property Code section 202.011 stops a property owners' association from banning shingles built to resist wind and hail, or to save energy, if they look like the shingles it already allows. The association can weigh in on appearance, not the rating.

Questions to ask a roofer in Conroe

About the house

  • Am I inside the City of Conroe or in unincorporated Montgomery County?
  • If you find rotten sheathing or rafters, will you stop, tell me, and contact the city before you continue?
  • What is your per-sheet price for decking, and will I see photos of each damaged area?
  • Does my HOA need to approve the roof first, and will you prepare the request?

About the company

  • Can your insurance agent send me a current certificate of liability coverage?
  • Who registers the shingle warranty, and what does your own workmanship warranty cover?
  • Which Conroe roofs can I drive past?
  • On an insurance job, what does your contract say about my deductible?
