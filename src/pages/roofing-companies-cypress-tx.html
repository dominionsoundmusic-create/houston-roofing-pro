Cypress is a place with no city limits

Roofing in Cypress, TX starts with a fact many newcomers miss: there is no City of Cypress. The name covers a wide, unincorporated swath of northwest Harris County, served by the county, the school district and a patchwork of utility districts, and it has no municipal building department of its own.

The name itself shows how the area grew. Cy-Fair comes from the 1930s merger of the Big Cypress school and Fairbanks High School into one district, and for decades the land around them stayed rural. Then the subdivisions arrived in waves, first along Highway 6 and FM 529, later out along US 290 and the Grand Parkway. That is why one Cypress street can hold a house on its third roof and the next can hold a roof that has seen only a few summers.

May 16, 2024: the evening Cypress lost its roofs

Most of greater Houston remembers that Thursday for the derecho downtown. In Cypress it also brought tornadoes. Here is the sequence, drawn from National Weather Service findings as reported by ABC13 and KHOU.

  1. Early evening, a tornado touches down. The weather service's survey team rated it an EF-1 with peak winds of 110 mph. Its path ran about 0.77 miles and reached up to 100 yards across.
  2. A 55 and over community takes a beating. ABC13 reported significant storm damage at The Heritage at Towne Lake, a 55 and over community, where neighbors spent the next days cleaning up torn roofing and debris.
  3. The same storm crosses the whole metro. The weather service's Houston/Galveston office documented a swath of winds above 100 mph through the Houston area that night, the event now called the May 2024 derecho.
  4. Three months later, a second Cypress tornado. On August 13, 2024, after reviewing video, the weather service confirmed that another EF-1 had touched down that evening in the Bridgeland area, according to KHOU.
  5. Seven weeks after the derecho, Beryl. Hurricane Beryl came ashore near Matagorda on July 8, 2024 and left more than two million CenterPoint Energy customers without power across the region, a second round of tropical wind on roofs that had just been through May.

The lesson for a Cypress roof is that wind damage is often partial. A house a few streets from a tornado path can lose seal strips and ridge caps without anything obvious from the curb, and the first sign may be a ceiling stain in the next heavy rain. If your roof went through 2024 and nobody has looked at it closely since, that is worth a call. Many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call.

A young roof that leaks is a warranty question first

When a roof fails in its first few years, the cause is usually one detail done badly, not shingles that wore out. That changes the order of calls. Read the builder warranty that came with the house, note what it covers and for how long, and look up the shingle maker's warranty, which may depend on how the roof was installed. A roofer who works Cypress should ask the age of the house before quoting and tell you plainly if the builder ought to see the roof first.

An older roof is a decking question

On a house that has been reroofed before, what matters most is underneath. A second layer laid over the first hides soft decking and makes the next replacement heavier work. Ask for photos of the bare deck once the old layers are off, before new underlayment covers it. Those photos are often the only record of what was found, because no city inspector stands in the driveway in Cypress.

Permits, the county and your HOA

With no city, building permits in Cypress come from Harris County, through the engineer's office. Its permits FAQ links the need for a permit to development and gives that word a very wide meaning that takes in buildings and almost any other change made to a property. Its online application list covers residential construction. What its pages do not do is address re-roofing by name, so this page will not tell you a replacement is exempt or that it is not. If you want certainty for your address, ask the county's permits office directly, and have the roofer write into the contract which permits, if any, it will pull.

In practice the rule book most Cypress homeowners meet is the one from their homeowners association. Master-planned communities usually require architectural approval for a change of roof color or material, and a replacement in a different shade is a change. Texas Property Code section 202.011 stops an association from prohibiting impact-resistant or energy-saving shingles that look like the ones it already permits, which is useful in a part of the county where spring storms bring hail. A 1.25 inch hail report near Cypress on December 24, 2024, listed in StormerSite's log compiled from weather service reports, is a reminder that it is not only a spring problem.

Cypress is well inland, outside the Texas Department of Insurance's designated catastrophe area, so no TDI windstorm inspection applies to a roof here.

Questions to ask a roofer working in Cypress

  • Which permits, if any, will you pull for this address with Harris County, and will you put that in writing?
  • Will you prepare the HOA architectural submittal, and which colors does my section allow?
  • After the May 2024 storms, did you check for broken seal strips and creased shingles, and can I see your photos?
  • If my house is under ten years old, do you think the builder or shingle maker should see this first?
  • Will you tear off every existing layer, and what is your written price per sheet of decking?
  • Can your insurer send me a certificate of liability insurance directly?
  • What workmanship warranty do you give in writing, and will you register the shingle maker's warranty for me?
