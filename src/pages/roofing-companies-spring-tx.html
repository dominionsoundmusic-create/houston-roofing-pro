A big community with no city limits

Roofing in Spring, TX works differently from roofing in a city, because Spring is not one. It is a census place and a mailing address that stretches across unincorporated Harris County and, north of Spring Creek, into Montgomery County.

The U.S. Census Bureau counted 62,559 people in the Spring census-designated place in 2020, up from 54,298 in 2010, and the Spring mailing address covers far more ground than that. The community started as a trading post William Pierpont set up on Spring Creek in 1838, according to the Texas State Historical Association. German farmers came in the 1840s, the Houston and Great Northern Railroad arrived in 1871, and by the early 1900s Spring had a roundhouse and a busy switchyard. When the rail jobs left, the old houses near the tracks were restored and turned into the shops of Old Town Spring.

Most of today's Spring was built much later, in subdivisions spreading out from FM 1960, Kuykendahl Road and Interstate 45. That spread is why the age and makeup of a Spring roof can vary so much from one street to the next.

The deck question on older Spring houses

Neighborhoods such as Champion Forest, which Community Impact describes as about 1,469 single-family homes, and Memorial Northwest went up decades ago, many of their houses in the late 1970s and 1980s. On houses that age, the part of the roof nobody sees can change the price of a new one more than the shingles do.

What the code says

The International Residential Code treats wood shakes and asphalt shingles differently. Section R905.8 lets shakes go on spaced sheathing: rows of boards at least 1 by 4 inches, set apart to line up with the nails. Section R905.2.1 says asphalt shingles must be fastened to solidly sheathed decks. Shingles nailed over gaps have nothing to hold the nails and can sag between boards.

What that means on your house

If a house was first roofed with shakes, or if a later shingle roof was laid straight onto those boards, a proper replacement covers the whole roof in plywood or OSB before the underlayment goes down. That is a large line on a quote. A roofer can see which kind of deck you have from the attic in a few minutes, and it should show you photos before you sign.

Ask these before the tear-off

  • Did you look in the attic, and is my deck solid sheets or spaced boards?
  • Does the quote include new sheathing over the whole roof, or a per-sheet price for what you find?
  • Will you photograph the bare deck before it is covered, and send me the photos?
  • How many layers of old roofing are up there now, and does the price include removing all of them?

Two counties, almost no roof permits

Because Spring has no city government, nobody issues a Spring roofing permit and no city inspector climbs up after the job. What applies depends on which side of Spring Creek you live on.

Storms that hit Spring roofs

Spring is too far inland for storm surge, but recent years show how much damage wind alone does when pines and water oaks lean over nearly every lot.

DateWhat happened in and around Spring
May 16, 2024A derecho crossed the Houston area. ABC13 later described 60 to 80 mph gusts and two EF1 tornadoes in northwest Harris County neighborhoods.
July 8, 2024Hurricane Beryl. ABC13 reported trees still resting on houses in Spring's Timber Lane subdivision weeks later, and KHOU told of a Spring couple living in an RV after a 60-foot tree crushed their home. The Texas A&M Forest Service estimated that about half of the Houston area's trees took some hit.
November 24, 2025A tornado tore through Klein-area neighborhoods. The National Weather Service rated it EF-2, ABC13 reported about 100 homes damaged in northwest Harris County, and Associated Press coverage described torn roofs, garages shifted and smashed windows.

The quiet damage matters as much as the dramatic kind. A strong gust can break the seal strips on a row of shingles without tearing any off. From the street the roof looks fine, and the first sign is a stain on a bedroom ceiling a few rains later. If your roof went through 2024 and the 2025 tornado season without anyone looking at it, a look now is reasonable. Many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call.

Why Spring homeowners call the line

A quote that jumped for decking

The first roofer said shingles; the second said shingles plus a full new deck. Both may be honest, if one looked in the attic and one did not. Callers want a roofer that will check the boards and explain the difference with pictures.

A tree came through after the tornado or Beryl

A limb through the decking turns a roof job into a structural repair, with rafters, sheathing and the ceiling below. Spring callers with that kind of damage want a company that will close the opening against rain first and then rebuild the damaged section properly, with the structural work spelled out.

No inspector to catch mistakes

In a city, a permit inspection is a second set of eyes. On county land in Spring there is none. Homeowners who know that want a roofer that writes the full scope down and sends photos of the work at each stage.

An HOA approval to file first

Champion Forest, Memorial Northwest and many other Spring subdivisions carry deed restrictions. A roofer that knows the local associations can tell you what paperwork comes before the tear-off and which shingle colors usually get approved.

What a Spring re-roof usually involves

Most houses in Spring carry asphalt shingles, and most owners replace them with shingles again. The work itself is routine for any roofer. The decisions that change how long the new roof lasts are these:

  1. Deck first. Confirm solid sheathing, or price new sheathing, before choosing anything else.
  2. Pick the shingle for shade and wind. Lots under heavy canopy stay damp and grow dark algae streaks, so an algae-resistant shingle is worth asking about. Wind ratings differ between products; ask what the shingle is rated for and how it must be nailed to earn that rating.
  3. Fix the ventilation. Older Spring attics often have too little intake at the eaves. A hot, poorly vented attic shortens shingle life.
  4. Mind the trees. Ask which limbs over the roof should be trimmed before the new roof goes on, and who does that work.
  5. Register the warranty. Shingle makers' warranties often depend on registration and approved installation. Ask who registers it and get the paperwork.
