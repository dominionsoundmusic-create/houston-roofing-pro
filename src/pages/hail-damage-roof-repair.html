Hail damage repair in Houston starts with finding the hits

Hail damage repair in Houston is mostly detective work before it is roofing work. The question is not whether hail fell on your street, but whether it broke your shingles, on which slopes, and how many.

Spring storms bring hail across the metro most years. A roof that took a hard hit often looks normal from the yard, because the damage is a bruise or a patch of missing granules a couple of inches across. Up close, those marks tell a roofer whether the shingle's mat was cracked, whether the damage is spread across the roof, and whether a repair or a new roof is the honest answer.

What hail does to a roof

The Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety (IBHS), an insurance industry research group, publishes a short guide to telling hail damage from ordinary wear. These are the signs it and shingle makers describe, and what each one means.

SignWhat it looks likeWhat it means
Random impact marksRound dark spots scattered across a slope with no patternIBHS lists a random pattern as a mark of hail. Wear and blistering tend to look more uniform.
BruisingA soft spot that gives when pressed, often under a dark markThe mat underneath may be fractured even though the surface looks intact.
Granule loss at the hitA patch of bare asphalt where the stones were knocked offThe granules shield asphalt from the sun. Without them, that spot ages fast in Houston heat.
Cracks and exposed matSplits radiating from a hit, or the mat showing throughA broken shingle. A shingle maker's technical bulletin notes this can also appear later as the roof ages.
Damaged ridge capsCracked or crushed caps along the peakIBHS calls ridge caps especially vulnerable because they sit on top and take direct hits.
Dents in soft metalDimpled gutters, vents, flashing and air conditioner finsSupporting evidence that hail of a certain size fell at your house. IBHS suggests checking it.

IBHS also warns that loose granules alone do not prove hail. Edge wear, normal aging, manufacturing defects and heavy rain all shed granules. That is why a careful roofer looks for the combination: random hits, bruises and dented metal from the same storm date.

Small hail counts too

In 2025 IBHS published research in the journal Frontiers in Materials on hailstones under an inch across. Its testers found that concentrations of this smaller hail can significantly reduce a shingle's resistance to later large hail and speed up the natural aging of asphalt shingles, and Insurance Journal reported on the study and the debate it started over roof claims. A Houston roof that has sat through several small hail events is not necessarily undamaged.

What you can check from the ground

None of this needs a ladder. Stay off the roof: wet shingles are slick and a hail-damaged roof gives no warning.

Walk the outside

  • Dents along the gutters and downspouts
  • Dimpled metal roof vents you can see from the yard
  • Bent fins on the outdoor air conditioner unit
  • Spatter marks on fences, decks and siding

Look at the ground

  • Heaps of granules at downspout outlets
  • Pieces of shingle or ridge cap in the beds
  • Shredded leaves and dented cars on the street
  • Photos of hailstones next to a coin, with the date

Write it down

  • The date and time the hail fell
  • Photos of every dent, taken from the ground
  • Any new ceiling stains inside
  • Neighbors who are getting roofs inspected

Dented metal does not prove the shingles broke, and clean gutters do not prove they did not. It tells you whether a close inspection is worth booking. Keep your notes with the storm claim checklist.

How a roofer inspects for hail

  1. Confirm the storm. Match the date you remember with weather records for your area. National Weather Service storm data lists hail reports by place and size.
  2. Check the soft metal first. Vents, flashing and gutters show what size hail fell and from which direction.
  3. Mark test squares. A common industry method, described in home inspector training, is to mark a 10 by 10 foot square (100 square feet) on each slope, away from tree cover, and count the hits that broke shingles inside it.
  4. Feel for bruises. A hit that looks like a dark spot may be soft underneath. The roofer presses and photographs each one with a marker beside it.
  5. Check the attic and the edges. Ridge caps, hips and the attic side of the deck, for anything that let water in.
  6. Hand you the record. Photos by slope, the hit counts, the storm date and a written opinion on repair or replacement. You keep it whether or not you file.

Test squares have limits. A 2026 Claims Journal article points out that a square is a sample, and that placing it where damage looks worst or access is easiest can skew the count. Ask where the squares went and why, and be present if an adjuster inspects.

Repair or replace after hail

Hail driven by wind often hits the slopes that faced the storm much harder than the others, which is why a roofer counts hits slope by slope. That shapes the answer.

When a repair can make sense

A few broken shingles on one slope, a cracked ridge cap or a dented vent on a roof that is otherwise in good shape. The roofer replaces what broke and should show you how close the new shingles come to the old color and style. A repair is not the answer if the shingle line is discontinued and the patch would stand out.

When a new roof is worth pricing

Broken shingles on several slopes, bruising spread across the roof, or an older roof whose shingles were already brittle. Patching a heavily hit roof shingle by shingle tends to leave the next leak waiting. If insurance may be involved, ask the insurer how it decides between repairing a slope and replacing the roof, and how its policy treats shingles that cannot be matched.

Class 4 shingles on a new roof

Shingles are tested for impact under the UL 2218 standard and rated Class 1 to Class 4, with Class 4 the most resistant. Shingle maker GAF explains that a Class 4 product withstands a 2 inch steel ball dropped from 20 feet. The Texas Department of Insurance says insurers may give a credit for impact-resistant roofing, with the amount for each class set company by company and Class 4 getting the largest. After installation the roofer fills out TDI form PC068 for your insurer, and TWIA requires the UL class to be labeled on the product. In 2026 TDI issued a bulletin encouraging insurers to offer supported discounts and tell policyholders about them. Ask your insurer what credit it gives, if any, before you choose.

If you live under a homeowners association, Texas Property Code section 202.011 limits an association's power to block shingles designed mainly to resist wind and hail, as long as they look like the shingles already allowed and are at least as durable.

Questions to ask a roofer about hail damage

About the inspection

  • Which storm date do you think caused this, and how did you check it?
  • Where did you put the test squares, and how many hits broke the shingle in each?
  • Can I see close photos of the bruises with a marker beside them?
  • Is any of this granule loss just age, and how can you tell?

About the work

  • Can the damaged shingles be matched, and what if they cannot?
  • Is the shingle you are quoting rated Class 4 under UL 2218, and will you fill out TDI form PC068?
  • Will you send your certificate of liability insurance and pull any permit needed?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover, in writing?

Any claim is filed by you, with your insurer, not by the roofer. A roofer may document the damage and meet the adjuster at your request, but under Texas Insurance Code section 4102.163 it may not act as your public adjuster on a property it works on.
