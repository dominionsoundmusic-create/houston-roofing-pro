The short answer, with its sources

There is no single roof replacement cost for Houston, TX, but two well-known published figures give you a frame. The 2024 Cost vs. Value report from the Journal of Light Construction listed $25,996 for its model asphalt shingle reroof in the Houston market. HomeAdvisor's 2025 national cost guide puts a typical project at about $5,900 to $13,400.

Those numbers are far apart because they describe different jobs. The Cost vs. Value model is a big, fully specified 30-square roof with skylights, priced the way a remodeling company would bill it. The HomeAdvisor range is built from what homeowners report paying, across all sizes of house. Your roof could land inside, below or above either one.

Houston Expert Roofers does not quote, sell or install roofs. It is a free phone line that puts you through to an independent, locally owned roofing company, and that company measures your roof and gives you its own written price. Real quotes vary, sometimes by thousands of dollars between two honest roofers, and this page explains why.

The Cost vs. Value model roof

The Journal of Light Construction's project description is worth reading because it shows what a complete reroof includes. The model job removes the existing roofing down to bare wood sheathing and installs 30 squares (3,000 square feet of roof surface) of fiberglass asphalt shingles weighing at least 235 pounds per square with at least a 25-year warranty, over new felt or synthetic underlayment, with galvanized drip edge and aluminum flashing. The model house has a rectangular hip roof, two skylights and a vented ridge. That is a larger roof than many Houston homes carry, and skylights add labor, which helps explain why the figure sits so far above the HomeAdvisor average.

The HomeAdvisor range

HomeAdvisor's range is wider and lower because it blends small and large homes, simple and complex roofs, and everything from basic shingles up. It is useful as a reality check on a quote, not as a target. The page itself notes that premium materials push the per-square-foot figure far higher than the typical band.

Use both the same way: as a sense of scale. The figure that counts is the one a roofer writes after measuring your roof, counting your layers and walking your attic.

How a roofer turns your roof into a number

Measuring in squares

Roofers price by the square because shingles are sold that way. The count comes from measuring every slope, by hand on the roof or from aerial measurement software, then adding a waste allowance for the shingles cut off at hips, valleys and edges. A hip roof with many facets wastes more than a plain gable. Ask what waste percentage the quote uses; two roofers measuring the same roof should land close to each other on squares, and a big gap is a question worth asking.

Pitch, stories and the summer

Pitch is the rise of the roof over a 12-inch run. A low slope can be worked on foot; a steep one needs roof jacks, harnesses and more time per square. In a Houston summer, crews also start early and stop when the shingles get too hot to handle well, which stretches a big job.

Tear-off and the decking you cannot see

Some homes still carry two layers of shingles from an old overlay. Both have to come off for a proper replacement, and every layer adds labor and disposal. Under them is the deck. A per-sheet decking price protects you both ways: the roofer is paid fairly for rot it finds, and you are not paying for an allowance of sheets that were never needed.

Material choices and what they change

Architectural asphalt shingles

The common choice on Houston homes and the material in the Cost vs. Value model. Thicker, with a layered look, and usually carrying a longer manufacturer warranty than the flat three-tab shingle. Price varies by brand line, weight and color.

Impact-resistant shingles

Shingles tested for hail resistance cost more up front. The Texas Department of Insurance says insurers set any discount for impact-resistant roofing company by company, and each decides which test results and paperwork it accepts. Ask your insurer before you choose, and keep the roofer's paperwork.

Metal roofing

Standing seam and other metal systems cost well above shingles and need installers who do that work regularly. The 2025 Cost vs. Value report shows metal recouping a smaller share of its cost at resale than asphalt shingles nationally. The metal roofing page covers the tradeoffs.

Tile and low-slope roofs

Clay or concrete tile is heavy and slow to install, and the framing has to carry it. Low-slope sections over porches or additions need a membrane, not shingles, and are often priced separately. See flat roof repair for those materials.

Permits, disposal and the coast

Inside the City of Houston, the Houston Permitting Center requires a residential roofing permit for a re-roof or an overlay, while roof covering work of 100 square feet or less in total is exempt. The permit fee is part of the job cost, and the roofer should pull it in its own name. Suburban cities set their own rules and fees, and unincorporated county areas follow different rules again, so ask the roofer what applies at your address. Each town page covers the local rule.

Disposal is the other line people forget. A whole roof of old shingles fills a roll-off dumpster, and the hauling and landfill charges are real money. A quote should say that removal and cleanup are included, including a magnet sweep of the yard and driveway for nails.

Near the coast there is one more step. In the Texas Department of Insurance's designated catastrophe area, which in greater Houston takes in Brazoria, Chambers and Galveston counties and parts of eastern Harris County, roof work has to meet the windstorm code and be inspected while it is under way so the home can get its compliance certificate. That inspection schedule can affect timing and cost, so ask about it up front.

Why two quotes for the same roof differ by thousands

It is rarely the shingle. When two itemized bids are far apart, the difference almost always sits in one of these lines. Lay the quotes next to each other and go down the list.

  • Decking. One bid includes a per-sheet price, another quietly assumes no rot, and a third builds in a fixed allowance. The low bid can become the high one on the first morning.
  • Flashing. New step flashing, counterflashing and pipe boots cost more than reusing old metal, and reused flashing is a common place for a new roof to leak.
  • Underlayment and barriers. Synthetic underlayment, and an ice and water barrier in valleys and around penetrations, add cost and protect against wind-driven Gulf Coast rain.
  • Ventilation. A ridge vent and balanced intake at the soffits help a hot attic. One quote may include that work and another may not touch it.
  • Drip edge, starter strip and ridge cap. Small items, but each is real material and labor, and leaving one off saves the bidder money, not you.
  • Permit and inspection. Whether the price includes the permit, and who meets the inspector.
  • Warranty. A one-year workmanship promise and a ten-year one are different products. So is a basic manufacturer warranty versus an enhanced one that requires the maker's full system of components.

Two lump-sum numbers cannot be compared. Two line-item estimates can. If a roofer will not itemize, that alone tells you something. The roof replacement page describes each component of a complete job.

Paying for it: schedule and financing questions

A sensible payment schedule

  • A modest deposit when you sign, stated in the contract
  • Possibly a payment when materials arrive at your house
  • The balance after the roof is finished, you have walked the property, and any permit inspection has passed
  • A paid invoice and the warranty paperwork in your hand at the end
  • Never the full price in cash before work starts

If the roofer offers financing

  • Who is the lender, and is the loan with them or with the roofer?
  • What are the rate, the APR, the monthly payment and the total repaid?
  • Is there a penalty for paying it off early?
  • Does the loan place a lien on my home?
  • Is the roofing price the same if I pay cash or use my own loan?

Get every promise in writing, including start date, cleanup and warranty terms. If anyone who knocked on your door pressures you to sign on the spot, slow down and get a second opinion.

When insurance is part of the cost

If the roof was damaged by hail or wind, your homeowners policy may cover part of the replacement, but how much depends on the policy, not the roofer. The Texas Department of Insurance explains that replacement cost coverage pays up to the cost to replace the roof at current prices, while actual cash value coverage subtracts depreciation for the roof's age and wear. TDI notes that some insurers move older roofs to actual cash value at renewal. Either way, your deductible comes off first, and with a percentage wind and hail deductible that can be several thousand dollars.

On a replacement cost claim the insurer usually pays in two parts: a first check for the estimate minus depreciation and the deductible, then the withheld depreciation once it sees the bill for the finished work. Ask your insurer what your policy says before you sign a roofing contract, and read the roof insurance claims guide for the details.

What a new roof returns when you sell

The 2025 Cost vs. Value report estimates that an asphalt shingle replacement recoups 68% of its cost at resale nationally. In the 2024 report's Houston figures, the estimated resale value was $12,350 on the $25,996 model job, a little under half. A new roof also matters to buyers' inspectors and insurers in ways a resale percentage does not capture, so treat these as one input among several, not a reason to replace a roof with good years left. The guide how long does a roof last in Houston helps with that decision.
