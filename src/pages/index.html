A phone line for Houston roofing, not another roofing company

Roofing in Houston comes with a long list of choices: hundreds of companies, door-knockers after every storm, and quotes that are hard to compare. This site and its phone line exist to make the first step simple.

Houston Expert Roofers is a free phone number plus the guides on this website. It does not own ladders or trucks, does not inspect or price roofs, and holds no license or certification. Dial it at any hour and an automated assistant gets a short description of the roof problem and your neighborhood, then transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofing company that works in your area.

That company does everything that follows: it looks at the roof, explains what it found, puts its own price in writing, carries out the job and sends its bill to you, or to your insurer if that is what you arrange. What Houston weather does to a roof

Houston roofs take heat, humidity, wind and hard rain, often in the same week. Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, and the storms that do the most roof damage are not always hurricanes.

  • Straight-line wind. On May 16, 2024, a derecho sent a swath of winds over 100 mph through the Houston metro, according to the National Weather Service, lifting and tearing shingles across the region.
  • Tropical storms and hurricanes. Hurricane Beryl made landfall near Matagorda on July 8, 2024 as a Category 1 storm. Its wind brought down trees and limbs across the metro and left more than two million CenterPoint Energy customers without power.
  • Hail. Spring thunderstorms bring hail that can bruise shingles without leaving marks you can see from the driveway. See hail damage roof repair.
  • Heat and humidity. Sun and heat dry out shingles and the rubber seals around vents, and wet decking dries slowly in Gulf Coast humidity. See how long a roof lasts in Houston.

Before you hire any roofer in Texas

The line does not vet, rate or license anyone, so these checks are yours to make, whichever company you end up hiring.

What you can check

  • A certificate of liability insurance, sent to you by the insurer or agent
  • A written contract with tear-off, decking price per sheet, underlayment, flashing and ventilation spelled out
  • A permit pulled in the roofer's name where your city requires one
  • Roofs the company finished near you that you can drive past
  • The shingle maker's warranty registration after the job

What Texas law says

  • There is no state roofing license in Texas.
  • A contractor may not waive, rebate or absorb your insurance deductible.
  • A contract of $1,000 or more paid with insurance money must carry a notice that you must pay the deductible.
  • A roofer may not act as your public adjuster on a property it works on.
  • In the coastal catastrophe area, most roof work must pass a Texas Department of Insurance windstorm inspection.
