Near the water, outside the windstorm map

Roofing in Webster, TX sits in an unusual spot. The city is in Harris County on the Clear Lake side of greater Houston, close enough to Galveston Bay to feel the same storms, yet it is not part of the state's designated catastrophe area. Two of its neighbors are in it: League City, across the line in Galveston County, and Seabrook, the part east of State Highway 146, as one of the five Harris County cities on the state's list.

That single fact changes a lot. A Webster re-roof does not need the Texas Department of Insurance windstorm inspection, and it does not end with a WPI-8 certificate. A Webster home also cannot turn to the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association for wind and hail coverage, because TWIA only insures property inside the designated area. The rules that do apply come from the City of Webster's Building Division and from your own homeowners policy.

Houston Expert Roofers does none of the work. It is a free phone line. An automated assistant answers any hour, asks what the roof is doing and where in Webster you are, and transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofing company that takes calls here.

Webster permits and contractor paperwork

The City of Webster's Building Division runs permits through an online portal and keeps a contractor registration process. Here is what that means for a roof job, step by step.

  1. Confirm you are inside the City of Webster. A Webster mailing address is not proof. Your Harris Central Appraisal District record lists the taxing jurisdictions for your property.
  2. Ask whether the job needs a permit. The Building Division can tell you what a re-roof or a repair of your size requires. Ask the roofer the same question and compare the answers.
  3. Check the roofer's paperwork with the city. Webster's contractor registration form asks specialty contractors for their license, proof of insurance and business details, and says no permit becomes effective until the required license and certificate of insurance are on file. There is no registration fee. Ask the roofer whether its paperwork is on file in Webster.
  4. File online. The city takes permit applications through its online permit portal. The roofer should apply in its own name and give you the permit number.
  5. Keep the record. Hold on to the permit, the contract, the invoice and the shingle warranty registration. A buyer or an insurer may ask for them later.

The city lists 281-338-2925 for help with online permit applications. Rules and forms change, so confirm the current requirement with the Building Division before work starts.

Wind and hail coverage without TWIA

Along the bay, many owners know the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association as the wind and hail insurer of last resort. TWIA says a property must be located in the catastrophe area designated by the commissioner of insurance to be eligible. In Harris County that area covers only parts of La Porte, Morgan's Point, Pasadena, Seabrook and Shoreacres, inside city limits and east of State Highway 146. Webster is not on that list.

So for a Webster home, wind and hail damage is a question for your regular homeowners policy. Before a storm, ask your agent three things: whether wind and hail carry their own deductible, whether that deductible is a percentage of the home's insured value or a flat amount, and whether the roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value. Those answers decide what a roof claim looks like long before any roofer arrives.

After a storm, call the insurer first and let the adjuster see the damage before permanent repairs. A roofer can photograph the roof and explain what it found, but you file the claim.

What recent storms mean for Webster roofs

Being outside the windstorm map does not mean being out of the wind. Hurricane Beryl made landfall near Matagorda on July 8, 2024, and as it moved inland Community Impact reported over 2.2 million CenterPoint Energy customers in the dark across the region. In May of that year, the National Weather Service office in League City recorded a derecho with winds above 100 mph cutting through parts of the metro. Shingles that lost their seal in either event can look normal from the parking lot and still leak in the next sideways rain.

Signs worth a call: shingles or pieces of ridge cap in the yard, a new ceiling stain, a drip around a light fixture, or dented metal vents. Many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call.
