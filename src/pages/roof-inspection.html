A roof inspection tells you what you actually have

A roof inspection in Houston is usually prompted by a storm, a home sale or a stain on the ceiling. Done well, it answers three questions: is the roof letting water in, is there storm damage worth reporting to your insurer, and how much life does it have left.

A careful inspection covers the roof surface and the attic underneath it, and it ends with photos and a written report you can keep whether or not you hire anyone. This page explains what an inspection should include, what a good report looks like, how a roofer's inspection differs from a home inspector's or an insurance adjuster's, and how often to have one.

What a thorough roof inspection covers

An inspection that stays on the driveway, or that never goes into the attic, sees only part of the roof. These are the areas a careful roofer works through.

The roof surface

Shingle condition across every slope: cracked, creased, lifted or missing pieces, curling edges, bare spots where granules have worn off, and hail bruises that show as soft spots or dark marks. On hail calls, many roofers mark out a test area on each slope and count hits, so the photos show more than one bad shingle.

Joints and penetrations

Pipe boots, exhaust vents, chimney and wall flashing, skylights and valleys. These joints are where most leaks start, so each one should be photographed. Thick caulk at a joint usually means an earlier patch. See where flashing goes.

Soft metal, gutters and edges

Gutters, downspouts, vent caps and drip edge show hail dents and wind bends clearly, which helps date storm damage. The inspector also checks whether drip edge is present and whether gutters are pulling away or clogged.

The attic

Water stains on the decking and rafters, daylight showing through, damp or matted insulation, rusted nail tips, and signs the attic is not venting well, such as extreme heat or moisture. An inspection that skips the attic is only half an inspection, so ask about it specifically.

The roofer may inspect on foot, from the eaves or with drone photos, depending on the pitch and conditions. You should never need to go up yourself. Stay on the ground and let the inspector show you the pictures afterward.

Roofer, home inspector or insurance adjuster?

Three different people may look at your roof, and they are answering different questions.

A roofer

Looks at whether the roof needs work, what kind and what it would cost, and may help you understand storm damage before you call your insurer. Remember the roofer who inspects is also hoping to do the work, so ask for photos of every finding, and get a second opinion on a big recommendation. Under Texas law a roofer may not act as your public adjuster on a property it works on.

A home inspector

Texas home inspectors are licensed by the Texas Real Estate Commission (TREC), at apprentice, real estate inspector and professional inspector levels. TREC's standards of practice require the roof covering to be inspected, walking it when that is safe, and flashing deficiencies reported. TREC has said a drone does not replace walking the roof when conditions allow. The inspector covers the whole house, so the roof gets a share of the visit.

An insurance adjuster

Sent by your insurer after you file a claim, the adjuster decides whether a covered event, such as hail on a certain date, caused damage and what the policy owes under its terms. A public adjuster, licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance, is someone you can hire to represent you on a claim. You may ask your roofer to be present when the adjuster inspects.

When to get a roof inspection

  • After hail or strong wind. Hail bruising is hard to see from the ground, and a lifted shingle can lie back down and look fine. Check your neighborhood's reports after a storm and, if hail fell, have the roof looked at and talk to your insurer.
  • Before buying a house. The home inspection covers the roof as part of the whole house. If it flags the roof, or the roof is older, a roofer's inspection gives you more detail before closing.
  • Before selling. Knowing the roof's condition first means the buyer's inspection is not where you learn about it.
  • When you see a stain. A new ceiling stain is worth a call even if it dries. See roof leak repair.
  • When deciding repair or replace. An inspection with photos makes it easier to compare quotes and spot an unnecessary recommendation.
  • On a schedule. Once a year is a sensible habit, more often for older roofs and roofs under trees.

Storms that are a reason to call

Severe thunderstorms do plenty of roof damage around Houston without a hurricane. The National Weather Service office in League City recorded winds above 100 mph in parts of the metro during the May 16, 2024 derecho, and Hurricane Beryl came ashore on July 8, 2024. A roof that went through both and has not been looked at since is a good candidate for an inspection. If you plan to file a claim, check your policy's deadline and filing rules with your insurer; claims with the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association must be filed within one year of the storm.

Questions to ask before and after the inspection

On the phone

  • Is there any charge for the inspection?
  • Will you check the attic as well as the roof?
  • Will I get a written report with photos, even if I do not hire you?
  • Can you send your certificate of liability insurance?

After the inspection

  • Which slopes have damage, and can I see each photo?
  • Is this storm damage, normal wear or an installation problem?
  • Can this be repaired, and what would a repair cost compared with a new roof?
  • If you recommend replacement, which findings is that based on?

A roofer worth hiring answers those plainly and is comfortable telling you a roof has years left.
