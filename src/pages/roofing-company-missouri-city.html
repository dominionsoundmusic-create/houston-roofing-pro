Missouri City checks the roofer before it checks the roof

When you compare roofing companies in Missouri City, TX, the city has already done part of the sorting. Its Permits and Inspections Division will not issue a permit to a contractor that has not registered, and registration asks for proof of general liability insurance.

That is unusual around Houston. Texas has no state license for roofers, and many towns ask nothing of a contractor before it starts tearing off shingles. Missouri City asks for paperwork first and then permits the reroof separately, so a homeowner here has a simple first question for any roofer: are you on the city's registered list, and under what name?

Houston Expert Roofers is not on that list, because it is not a contractor. It is a free phone number. A recorded assistant answers at any hour, takes a quick description of the roof and your part of Missouri City, and passes the call to an independent, locally owned roofing company. Everything after that, from the ladder to the invoice, is between you and that company.

From registered contractor to finished reroof

Each step below comes from Missouri City's own permit pages and forms. Together they show what a roofer working inside the city limits should already have in hand.

  1. The company registers with the city. Missouri City's contractor registration rules cover anyone who builds, repairs or renovates a structure in the city, and a contractor must be registered before a permit can be issued to it.
  2. It shows proof of liability coverage. The registration requirements include comprehensive general liability insurance from a company rated B+ or better, and the registration has to be renewed every year. That is a city filing, not a promise to you, so still ask for a certificate of insurance made out to you.
  3. It files a separate roof permit. The residential application treats roof repair or replacement as its own permit, apart from fences, driveways or mechanical work. Applications go through the city's online self service portal.
  4. It starts after the permit, not before. The city's application says fees are doubled when work begins before a permit is issued, and paying the higher fee does not excuse the work from meeting the code.
  5. It calls for the inspections. Inspections are requested through the same portal or by phone; the city says it does not take inspection requests by email, and requests made after 3 p.m. are scheduled for the next business day.

The city also publishes a list of active registered contractors with roofing as one of its license types. The copy found for this page was dated February 2025, so a company registered since then may not appear yet. If a name is missing, call Permits and Inspections at 281-403-8600 rather than guessing.

Two Missouri Citys under one name

Missouri City was incorporated on March 13, 1956, and much of its early housing followed the golf course. Development of Quail Valley began in 1969, and Lake Olympia, south of Quail Valley, and the neighborhoods along Texas Parkway and Cartwright Road grew up around it. The south side, toward the Brazos River, is a different story: Sienna and parts of Riverstone are newer master planned communities, and some of that land is not inside the city at all. Most of the city is in Fort Bend County; the city's own financial report puts roughly 10 percent of its population in Harris County.

The Sienna boundary is still moving

Community Impact reported in August 2023 that council annexed part of Sienna and added about 6,000 residents. In October 2026 the city outlined a plan to annex Sienna MUD No. 3 as well, with public hearings in November and council consideration tentatively set for December 7, 2026. If your house is in that district and your reroof is being scheduled around the turn of the year, ask the roofer to confirm with the city which rules apply on the day it files.

Recent weather on Missouri City roofs

Beryl, July 2024

Community Impact reported that 15 city facilities were damaged, about $2.18 million in all, with wind, roof or water damage to 15 of its 20 buildings and the City Centre at Quail Valley hit hardest. The city also waived permit fees for storm repairs. Roofs under those fallen limbs are worth a second look if no one has checked them since.

Hail in 2026

StormerSite, which compiles public and spotter reports, lists hail of more than an inch near Missouri City in the storm of April 30 into May 1, 2026, and Missouri City and Stafford both had hail reports during the March 15, 2024 storm. Field reports are not damage surveys, but hail that size can bruise shingles and dent vents.

No windstorm certificate here

Fort Bend County is not one of the coastal counties in the Texas Department of Insurance catastrophe area, and neither are the Harris County parts of the city. A Missouri City reroof answers to the city permit and inspections, not to the state windstorm inspection that Pearland's Brazoria County side faces.

Boundary questions in the south

A free call at any hour

The line costs you nothing. When the roofer is on another job, the details you gave (name, callback number, neighborhood, urgency) are relayed so it can ring you. Prices come only from the roofer. The company may pay the line a referral fee.

Deed restrictions, shingle choice and insurance

Sienna, Riverstone, Lake Olympia and many older sections have homeowners associations whose architectural committees approve roof color and style. Get the current guidelines from your association or its manager and hand them to the roofer, so the estimate names a product the committee will accept and the approval arrives before the permit is filed.

If hail is the worry, Texas Property Code section 202.011 stops an association from banning impact-resistant or energy-efficient shingles that look like the ones it already allows. Ask your insurer whether an impact-rated roof changes your premium, and ask the roofer for the product's rating in writing.

Questions to put to a Missouri City roofer

  • What name is your company registered under with the City of Missouri City, and can I find it on the city's list?
  • Will you file the separate roof permit in your name before any tear-off, and send me the permit number?
  • Is my lot inside the city, in annexed Sienna, or still outside the limits, and what changes for this job?
  • How many layers are on my roof now, and what is your written price per sheet for rotten decking?
  • What will you change in the attic ventilation, flashing and underlayment to meet the current code?
  • Who sends my HOA the request, and which approved shingle does this estimate use?
