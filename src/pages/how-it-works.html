Four steps from your call to a roofer

The line exists for one job: getting your call answered and in front of a local roofing company, quickly and at no cost to you.

What to have ready when you call

Where you are

Your city or ZIP code, and whether you are inside city limits if you know it. Permit and windstorm rules depend on it, and it decides which local company takes the call.

What you see

A drip, a ceiling stain, shingles in the yard, dented gutters, a limb on the roof, or simply an older roof you want looked at. Say whether water is coming in right now.

Your paperwork

If a storm caused it, your insurance policy and claim number if you have one. Photos you took from the ground and inside the house help the roofer understand the job.

What the line does not do

Not part of the line

  • Climb on, inspect or photograph roofs
  • Quote prices or compare estimates
  • Repair, replace or tarp anything
  • File, handle or advise on insurance claims
  • Vet, license, rate or insure roofing companies
  • Charge you anything

The roofing company's job

  • Coming out and inspecting the roof
  • Explaining what it found, ideally with photos
  • Giving you its own written estimate
  • Pulling any permit and doing the work
  • Its workmanship warranty and its bill to you
  • Documenting damage for your insurer if you ask

You are free to say no to any estimate, to get other quotes, or to hire someone you found another way. Calling the line does not commit you to anything.

After the call: habits that protect you

The line puts you in touch with a roofer; it cannot stand behind that roofer's work. These habits protect you with any company:

  1. Get it in writing. The scope, materials, decking price per sheet, permit, start date, payment schedule and workmanship warranty.
  2. Pay as the work happens. A deposit is common; most of the price up front is not.
  3. Keep the claim yours. You file it with your insurer. A roofer can document damage and meet the adjuster, but it may not act as your public adjuster.
  4. Pay your own deductible. Texas law makes it illegal for a contractor to waive, rebate or absorb it.
