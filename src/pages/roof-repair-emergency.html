When a roof problem is an emergency

A Houston emergency roof repair is about one thing: stopping water and weather from getting into the house tonight. The permanent fix usually comes later, once the roof is dry and the damage has been documented.

Emergency work costs more than a scheduled visit, so it helps to know which situations need it. These usually do:

  • Water running into the house, not dripping, and more rain on the way.
  • A tree or a large limb on or through the roof.
  • Daylight visible in the attic, or a section of deck torn open.
  • A ceiling that is bulging, sagging or cracking under trapped water.
  • Water near light fixtures, outlets or the attic wiring.
  • Any sag or bend in the roofline itself.

A slow drip into a bucket with a clear forecast is still worth a call, but it can usually wait for a regular appointment. The test is whether more water will come in before a roofer could get there in daylight.

Safe steps while you wait

The single most important rule: do not go up there. Roofs are slick in rain and at night, storm-damaged decking can give way, and a fall is far worse than a wet ceiling. Everything useful can be done from inside the house and from the ground.

Inside the house

  • Move furniture, electronics, rugs and papers away from the water.
  • Put buckets, pans or towels under every drip and empty them before they overflow.
  • Keep children and pets out of any room with a bulging or sagging ceiling, and close the door.
  • If water is near a light fixture or outlet, stay out of that room, keep switches untouched and tell the roofer and your utility on the phone.
  • Lay plastic sheeting over anything too heavy to move.

Outside and on the phone

  • Stay well clear of downed lines, and of any limb or fence touching them. Report them to CenterPoint Energy or 911.
  • Photograph the roof and the yard from the ground, and every wet spot inside, before anything is moved or covered.
  • Call a roofer to cover the opening, and let your insurance company know about the damage.
  • Write down the time the damage happened and the time you called each one.
  • Keep every receipt, from the tarp to a hotel night.

A ceiling holding water is dangerous to stand under. Leave it to the roofer or a water damage company to deal with, and keep people out of the room until then.

What a proper emergency tarp looks like

Tarping is the roofer's job, done from the roof with the right equipment. Knowing what a good one looks like helps you judge the work and explain it to your adjuster.

Signs of a sound tarp

  • It covers the whole damaged area with room to spare on every side.
  • It runs up and over the ridge where it can, so water flows across the top of it instead of under its upper edge.
  • It is held down with wood strips fastened along its edges, so wind cannot get a grip.
  • It lies tight and flat, without loose folds that catch wind or hold water.
  • The roofer photographs the damage before covering it and the tarp once it is on.

Signs of a weak one

  • A tarp laid loosely over the hole and weighted with bricks or tires.
  • The upper edge stops below the damage, so water runs straight beneath it.
  • Flapping corners, or a tarp too small for the opening.
  • No photos of what was underneath before it was covered.
  • No date given for the permanent repair.

A tarp is a temporary bridge, not a repair. Sun and wind wear it down, so ask the roofer, before it leaves, when it will come back to inspect properly and how long it expects this cover to hold.

Hiring in a hurry, without getting burned

Emergencies are when people sign things they would normally read twice. The Texas Department of Insurance warns that contractors who show up uninvited after a disaster sometimes present a contract as an estimate, and that a contract should never be signed with blank spaces. Pay for the emergency cover by check or card, not cash, and do not pay in full for the permanent repair up front. TDI also says that when a disaster has been declared, it is against the law for contractors from outside your area to ask for payment before they start work.

Separate the two jobs on paper. The emergency cover can be agreed on the spot. The permanent repair, which may be paid from an insurance claim, deserves a written, itemized estimate, a look at the roofer's certificate of liability insurance and time for your adjuster to see the roof.

Questions to ask on an emergency call

Before anyone drives out

  • What is your emergency or after-hours charge, and what does it cover?
  • Will the cover be a tarp fastened with wood strips and run over the ridge?
  • Do you take care of a limb on the roof, or do I need a tree crew too?
  • What is your business address in greater Houston?

Before the crew leaves

  • Can you send me the photos from before and after the tarp went on?
  • What did you see that I should tell my insurer?
  • When will you come back to inspect and quote the permanent repair?
  • Is the emergency charge credited against that repair?
