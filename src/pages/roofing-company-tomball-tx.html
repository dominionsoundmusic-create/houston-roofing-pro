Tomball the city, and the much larger Tomball address

Finding a roofing company in Tomball, TX starts with a question most people never think about: is the house inside the City of Tomball? The answer decides whether a city inspector ever sees your new roof.

The city itself is small. The 2020 census counted 12,341 people inside its limits. It grew from a railroad stop called Peck, which the city's own history says was renamed in 1907 for Thomas Ball, the Houston attorney who helped bring the rail line through. The town incorporated in 1933, around the time oil was found nearby, and that incorporation kept the City of Houston from later taking in its land. Old Town, the walkable Main Street district by the tracks, is still the heart of it.

The Tomball mailing address reaches much further: out along State Highway 249 into master-planned subdivisions such as Northpointe, and onto acreage roads where houses sit back from the pavement. Much of that is unincorporated Harris County, and north of Spring Creek it becomes Montgomery County. Same ZIP code, different rules.

The City of Tomball roofing permit, line by line

The city publishes a one-page residential roofing permit application, and its other permit forms add general rules. Here is what they say, and what each line means for you as the owner. Ask your roofer to show you the approved permit before tear-off starts.

What the city form saysWhat it means for a Tomball homeowner
Residential roofing permit fee: $50A small cost that belongs on the roofer's estimate. Ask whose name the permit is pulled in; it should be the roofing company's.
Commercial roofing needs a commercial building permitA shop on a commercial lot or a church roof is a different application. Say so on the call if the building is not a house.
Liability insurance of at least $500,000 combined single limit, city named as certificate holderThe roofer has to show coverage to the city. Ask for its certificate made out to you too, so you can call the insurance agent and confirm it is current.
Other city permit forms: penalty of double the fees for work without a required permitA roofer that says to skip the permit is putting the penalty on your property, and the record of the work is missing when you sell.
Other city permit forms: void if work does not start within six monthsAsk the city whether that window applies to roofing permits. After a busy storm season, a signed contract can sit for weeks, so get a start date in writing.

The form is not dated, so confirm the current fee with the City of Tomball before you rely on it. Outside the city limits there is no City of Tomball permit at all. On Harris County land, permits come from the county engineer's office and center on development and the floodplain, and Montgomery County sets its own rules, so ask the county or the roofer whether your address needs anything. Either way, with no city inspector on a county re-roof, the written contract and the roofer's photos become the main record of what was done. Read that contract closely.

Storms on record around Tomball

Tomball sits far enough inland that hurricanes arrive weakened, but the storms that do reach it bring hail and straight-line wind, and the tall pines that line so many lots turn wind into falling limbs. These events are documented.

Tomball's own gas service, if a tree comes down

One local difference matters when a limb lands on a house. The City of Tomball delivers natural gas to its customers, not only water. If you smell gas after a tree hits the roof, leave the house first, call 911 from outside, then call the city's leak line. The city lists (281) 290-1450 for weekdays during office hours and (281) 351-5451 for nights, weekends and holidays. Homes outside the city may have a different gas provider, which your bill names.

Why Tomball homeowners call the line

Hail on a roof that looks fine

Hail damage on asphalt shingles shows up as soft spots and lost granules, not holes. After a night like April 2020, a homeowner standing in the driveway cannot tell. A roofer walking the slopes, with photos, can. That is the most common reason a Tomball caller wants someone up there.

The house and the barn on one visit

Plenty of lots around Tomball carry a shop, carport or barn beside the house, and those often have exposed-fastener metal roofs. Screws back out, rubber washers dry and crack, and laps rust. If you want both roofs looked at, say so on the call, and ask for the house and the outbuilding to be priced as separate lines.

Pines over the roofline

Wooded lots are a large part of Tomball's appeal. They also drop needles into valleys and gutters, keep shaded slopes damp long enough to grow dark algae streaks, and send limbs down in every strong storm. A good roofer looks up at the trees before it quotes and tells you which limbs hang over the roof.

Not sure which rules apply

Northpointe carries a Tomball address but sits outside the city, while a house a few streets from Main Street is inside it. A crew from across town may not know the difference. A roofer that works here regularly settles the question in the first conversation, before anything is signed.

Shingle roofs and metal roofs on Tomball properties

Most houses here, old and new, carry asphalt shingles. On a replacement, the choices that matter most in this climate are the underlayment, the attic ventilation and, if hail worries you, whether to step up to an impact-resistant shingle. Those cost more, so ask your own insurer whether it treats them differently before you choose, and see the roof replacement cost guide for what drives the price.

Metal is common on outbuildings and increasingly chosen for houses. A metal roof on a house is usually a standing seam or a through-fastened panel, and the two behave very differently over time. The metal roofing page explains the difference and what to ask. If you are putting metal on a house inside the city, the permit rules above still apply.

Older homes near Old Town

Houses near the original downtown are older than the subdivisions along 249, and older roofs can hide older problems: a second layer of shingles laid over the first, plank decking with gaps between the boards, or a roof that was never properly vented. A careful roofer checks the attic and tells you before tear-off what it expects to find underneath, with a per-sheet price for any decking that has to be replaced.

Questions to ask a roofer in Tomball

About this house

  • Is my address inside the City of Tomball, and if so, will you pull the $50 roofing permit in your company's name?
  • Did you go into the attic, and can I see photos of the decking and the damage?
  • What is your price per sheet if decking has to be replaced?
  • Will you price my barn or shop roof as a separate line?

About your company

  • Can you send me a certificate of liability insurance with me named on it?
  • What does your workmanship warranty cover, and for how long, in writing?
  • Where are a few Tomball-area roofs you replaced that I can drive past?
  • How much deposit do you want, and when is the rest due?
