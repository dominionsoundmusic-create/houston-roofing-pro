A bay town where the windstorm rules apply to every street

Roofing in Texas City, TX starts with geography. The city lies on the west shore of Galveston Bay in Galveston County, and because the whole county is in the state's designated catastrophe area, there is no part of town where a new roof escapes the Texas windstorm inspection.

Texas City also lives with a piece of infrastructure most suburbs never think about: a ring of levees and floodwall built to hold back storm surge. People sometimes assume it shelters houses from the worst of a hurricane. It does a lot against water. It does nothing against wind, and wind is what tears up roofs.

This page covers both: what the levee really protects, how the city folds windstorm paperwork into its permits, and what to ask before a roofer starts work on your house. Houston Expert Roofers is a phone line only. It picks up at any hour, learns what is going on with the roof, and passes the call to an independent, locally owned roofing company that covers Texas City. That company handles the roof from there.

What the hurricane levee does, and what it leaves to the roof

The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' National Levee Database describes the Texas City Hurricane Flood Protection system as roughly 20.6 miles of levee and 1.35 miles of floodwall, with an average height of about 20 feet, 25 gravity structures and 2 pump stations. Construction began in 1962 and finished in April 1987, and the system reduces flood risk for about 45,000 people in Texas City and La Marque.

Storm surge from the bayHolds it back up to the height of the walls; the database warns that a surge larger than Ike's would most likely overtop itNot a roof question; ask your agent about flood insurance separately

Hurricane Ike tested the system in September 2008. The levee database says Ike produced the record surge loading on it, with the northeastern section loaded to its full height, and a Corps fact sheet says Ike's surge came within about two feet of overtopping a 21 foot section. Whatever the water did, the same storm's wind crossed the wall freely. That is the reason a Texas City roof is built and inspected as if the levee were not there.

How the City of Texas City handles roof paperwork

The Building Inspections Department works from 7800 Emmett F. Lowry Expressway, and permit applications run through the city's MyGov portal. The city's written rules are firm about who can be on a permit: a contractor has to be registered in MyGov first, which means supplying a driver's license, any state license the trade calls for and a certificate of liability insurance that lists the City of Texas City as certificate holder.

Texas City is also unusual in how closely it ties local permits to the state windstorm program. Its residential checklist asks for a completed TDI application, form WPI-1, to be attached when a permit request is submitted, and its handbook expects the windstorm certificate from the inspector or engineer by the framing inspection on new construction. The published checklists are written for new houses and additions; they do not spell out the re-roof process, so ask Building Inspections what a roof replacement on your house needs before the contract is signed.

Fees changed recently. The city says all residential permits follow an updated fee schedule from October 1, 2025, so the roofer's quote should show the current permit cost as its own line.

Which wind speed is the roof built for?

Older Texas windstorm maps split the coast into three zones, Seaward, Inland I and Inland II, and TDI's Galveston County page puts Texas City in Inland I, along with League City, Kemah and Dickinson. One of the city's own building guidelines still lists a design wind speed of 140 mph, measured as a 3 second gust, for that Inland I zone.

The state rules have moved on. TDI says that for work done on or after April 1, 2026, windstorm applications must be certified under the 2024 International Residential Code or International Building Code, and that the required wind speed is set for each structure by its location rather than by a county zone. The catastrophe area itself did not change. TDI also notes that most of the designated area now falls inside the windborne debris region, where flying debris is part of the design. For a homeowner the practical question is simple: ask the roofer which wind speed and which code edition it is designing your roof to, and to put the answer in writing with the products it will use.

Why Texas City homeowners call the line

A permit that needs a registered name

Because a roofer must be registered in MyGov before it can appear on a Texas City permit, an out of town crew that knocks after a storm may not be able to pull one. When you are put through, ask if that roofing company already has a MyGov registration.

WPI-1 before, WPI-8 after

The state paperwork brackets the job: the application goes in before work starts and the certificate comes out when the inspected work passes. A company that does coastal roofs regularly will talk about both without being prompted.

New subdivisions and older blocks

The city describes Lago Mar as a master-planned community, and new sections keep opening there and in subdivisions such as Beacon Point and Heron's Landing. Older parts of town hold houses whose roofs have already been through Ike, Harvey and Beryl. A new build under warranty and a roof on its third hurricane raise different questions.

Nothing to pay for the call

The line does not charge you, now or later. Price, contract and payment are all between you and the roofing company, and you can still get other bids. Companies may pay the line a referral fee.

Storms that hit Texas City roofs

  • September 13, 2008, Hurricane Ike. Landfall at Galveston as a Category 2 hurricane, a short drive across the bay. The levee held the surge out; the wind did not stop at the wall.
  • August 2017, Hurricane Harvey. A rain storm more than a wind storm here. The National Weather Service reported about 1,865 homes destroyed and 22,806 damaged by flooding across Galveston County.
  • July 8, 2024, Hurricane Beryl. Landfall near Matagorda, with the National Weather Service Houston/Galveston office reporting numerous trees and power lines down across the region and a top gust of 97 mph near the Brazos River in Brazoria County.

After any of these, the safe moves are the same: stay off the roof, keep away from lines on the ground, and record what you can see from the yard and from inside. In Texas City the power lines are Texas-New Mexico Power's, so report a downed line to TNMP at 888-866-7456, or to 911 if anyone is at risk.

Questions to ask before a Texas City roof job

  • Are you registered with the City of Texas City in MyGov, and will the permit be in your company's name?
  • Does my roof replacement need a city permit, and what does the current fee schedule charge for it?
  • Who files the WPI-1 with TDI, who schedules the windstorm inspection, and when will I get the WPI-8?
  • Which wind speed and code edition are you designing to, and which shingles or panels and fasteners meet it?
  • If my house is in Lago Mar or another subdivision with an association, who submits the roof to the HOA, and when?
  • Can you send your certificate of liability insurance, and what does your written workmanship warranty cover?

On the HOA point, Texas Property Code section 202.011 generally stops a property owners' association from banning shingles that resist wind and hail or improve energy efficiency, as long as they look like the shingles it already allows. The association can still ask for approval before work starts.
