A Friendswood roof starts with a county line

Roofing in Friendswood, TX comes with a question most homeowners never think about: which county is your house in? Friendswood lies partly in Galveston County and partly in Harris County, and the city's own re-roof permit form treats the two sides differently.

On the Galveston County side, the job falls inside the Texas Department of Insurance's windstorm catastrophe area, because Galveston County is one of the 14 first-tier coastal counties. There the city's form says the roofer submits a WPI-8 windstorm certificate and the city does no inspection of its own. On the Harris County side, Friendswood is not one of the Harris County cities in the catastrophe area, so there is no WPI-8, and the city does a final inspection instead. Two houses a few streets apart can end up with different paperwork for the same new roof.

What the city's re-roof application asks for

Friendswood's residential re-roof and siding permit application is short, and reading it tells you what the roofer should already know about your job. It asks for the square footage of roofing being replaced and the total cost or valuation of the work. Its inspection section is where the county line shows up: a WPI-8 on the Galveston County side, a city final inspection on the Harris County side.

The city's residential building requirements say permit applications must be approved and permits issued before any work is started, and submittals go through the online permit portal. A roofer that plans to tear off on Monday and "sort out the permit later" is not following that rule.

One practical use of all this: the city publishes monthly reports of the permits it issues, and re-roof permits are among the entries. If you are buying a Friendswood house with a "new roof", that report is a quick way to see whether the work was permitted.

Trees, shade and storms over Friendswood roofs

Where a Friendswood roof sits under tall pines or spreading oaks, the shade is pleasant in August but it changes how the roof ages. Needles and leaves pile up in the valleys, where two roof slopes meet, and hold water against the shingles. Shaded slopes stay damp longer after rain. Limbs that hang over the roof scrape shingles in the wind and can come down in a storm.

Storms have shaped Friendswood from the start. Quaker families founded it as a farming colony in 1895, and the city's history notes that their two-story Academy, which served as church, school and meeting place, was built from timber felled by the 1900 Galveston storm. The town incorporated in 1960 and grew into a commuter suburb once NASA's Manned Spacecraft Center opened nearby. In August 2017 Hurricane Harvey flooded about 2,410 single-family homes in Friendswood as Clear Creek rose, Community Impact reported. That was flood water, which a standard homeowners policy treats separately from wind damage to a roof.

Two recent storms tested those trees. On May 16, 2024 a derecho, which the National Weather Service office in League City documented, drove winds over 100 mph through parts of the metro area. Then Hurricane Beryl came ashore on July 8, 2024 near Matagorda as a Category 1 storm, and Community Impact reported more than 2.2 million CenterPoint Energy customers without power across the region. If a limb hit your roof in either storm, or you have not had the roof looked at since, ask a roofer to check the valleys, the flashing and the decking under any spot that was struck.
