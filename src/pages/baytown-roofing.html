Baytown roofing companies start with your county

Before comparing Baytown roofing companies, find out which county your roof is in. Few cities around Houston cross a county line the way Baytown does, with neighborhoods in both Harris County and Chambers County. Chambers is a first-tier coastal county under the Texas Department of Insurance windstorm program, Harris County Baytown is not, and that one fact changes the paperwork on a new roof.

The city also has a long memory of wind. It grew out of the oilfield and refinery towns of Goose Creek, Pelly and Baytown, which merged under one name in the late 1940s, on the low ground where Goose Creek and Cedar Bayou empty into the north end of Galveston Bay. Storms off the bay reach its roofs with nothing in the way.

Houston Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a roofer. When you call, an automated assistant asks about the roof and transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofing company that takes Baytown calls. That company inspects, prices and does the work.

Two counties, two sets of roof paperwork

Harris County side

The older Goose Creek side of the city, the original heart of Baytown, is in Harris County. A re-roof follows the City of Baytown's building rules, and the state windstorm program does not apply. Wind coverage comes from your regular homeowners policy.

Chambers County side

Here the TDI catastrophe-area rules apply. Roof work has to meet the windstorm building code and be inspected while it goes on, and the home receives a WPI-8 certificate of compliance that the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association relies on. City rules still apply inside city limits too.

The quickest check is the appraisal district: the Harris Central Appraisal District and the Chambers County Appraisal District each list only the properties in their own county. If yours turns up in Chambers, write the windstorm inspection and the WPI-8 into the roofing contract as deliverables, with the roofer named as the party that arranges them.

When Baytown wants a permit for roof work

Baytown draws its line at the deck, not at the shingles. The city's residential permit guidance puts each part of a roofing job in one of two columns.

Part of the jobCity permit?What to ask the roofer
Replacing roof shingles onlyNot required, per the city's listHow will you confirm the deck is sound before shingling over it?
Installing, replacing or repairing the roof deckRequiredWho applies for the permit if you find bad decking after tear-off?
Roofing with tar pots (hot work)Separate Fire Marshal processHave you applied to the Fire Marshal for this job?
Storm repairs after a declared disasterPermits still required where they apply; fees may be waivedIs a fee waiver in effect right now, and does it cover this job?

Applications, payments and inspection requests go through the city's Citizen Self Service portal, and Building Services answers questions at 281-420-6537. The city notes its list is not complete, so an unusual job is worth a quick call before work starts. A roofing company that works Baytown often should already know the answers.

Roof services Baytown homeowners ask for

Bay wind and salt-laden air wear on shingles, fasteners and flashing, and the older half of the city has roofs that have been patched more than once. These are the services most Baytown calls are about.

Baytown's storm record, from Alicia to Beryl

  1. Hurricane Alicia, August 1983. The U.S. Geological Survey says Alicia devastated Brownwood, a bayfront subdivision of roughly 500 homes that had already sunk from groundwater pumping. The Harris Galveston Subsidence District says the neighborhood was abandoned in the 1980s; it is now the Baytown Nature Center.
  2. A city that sank. Parts of the Baytown area dropped more than nine feet as groundwater was pumped out, which led the Legislature to create the subsidence district in 1975. The sinking has slowed sharply, but the lost ground means storm surge and heavy rain reach farther than they once did.
  3. The May 16, 2024 derecho. The National Weather Service describes a swath of winds over 100 mph across the Houston metro that afternoon, part of a fast line of storms that tore at roofs and trees across the region.
  4. Hurricane Beryl, July 8, 2024. Beryl knocked out power to more than two million CenterPoint Energy customers. Nine days later, Baytown announced it had waived permit fees for storm damage repairs.

Wind does its damage quietly. A shingle whose adhesive strip let go can settle back flat, so a roof can look fine from the street and still take water in the next sideways rain. If your roof has not had a close look since 2024, the storm damage page lists what to watch for from the ground.

Stay off the roof after a storm. Wet shingles are slick and storm-damaged decking can give way. Keep clear of downed power lines and report them to 911 or the electric utility named on your bill (Baytown spans two counties, so check which one serves you). Inside, move belongings away from any drip, set out buckets and towels, and keep people out of rooms where the ceiling sags. Photograph damage from the ground and indoors, then call your insurer.

Why Baytown homeowners call the line

The county question gets asked

Tell the assistant you are in Baytown and describe the roof. The company you are put through to can then ask the question that matters most here: Harris or Chambers.

Decking surprises are common

On houses that have been re-roofed before, nobody knows what the deck looks like until the shingles are off. A local roofer knows that soft boards trigger a city permit and should price them per sheet up front.

The bay does not keep office hours

Storms off Galveston Bay arrive at night as often as by day. The assistant answers at any hour, and if the roofer misses the call it passes on your name, number and the problem.

Nothing to pay the line

The call is free. The roofing company sets its own prices and invoices you, or your insurer if that is what you agree. Companies may pay the line a referral fee, and this page says so.

What a Baytown roofing contract should spell out

  • The county your house is in, and whether a windstorm inspection and WPI-8 are part of the job.
  • Tear-off to the deck, with a price per sheet for any decking replaced, and who files the city permit when that happens.
  • The underlayment, drip edge, flashing and ventilation by name, not "as needed."
  • Fasteners and flashing suited to salt air near the bay; ask what the shingle maker requires for coastal installs.
  • A certificate of liability insurance sent to you by the insurer or agent.
  • The workmanship warranty in writing, and who registers the shingle maker's warranty.
