A small city with its own roofing rules

Roofing in Stafford, TX works a little differently from the unincorporated land around it. Stafford is its own city, with a Development Services department that issues building permits, keeps a contractor registration, and publishes a monthly report of the permits it has issued, reroofs included.

The city is compact. It had 17,666 residents at the 2020 census and sits between Sugar Land and Missouri City along U.S. 59 and the Southwest Freeway corridor, mainly in Fort Bend County, with a sliver reaching into Harris. Stafford incorporated in 1956, and by the 1980s it had become a commuter suburb, so much of its housing dates from the 1970s through the 1990s and many of those roofs have already been replaced at least once.

Houston Expert Roofers is not a Stafford contractor or any kind of contractor. It is simply a number that costs nothing to dial. Whenever you ring, a recorded assistant collects a few details about the roof and your street, then transfers you to an independent, locally owned roofing company that answers Stafford calls. That company handles the roof from inspection to invoice.

How Stafford permits a reroof

These points come from the City of Stafford's own Development Services pages. Ask the roofer to walk you through each one before you sign.

Permits go through the online portal

The city says Development Services no longer accepts emails or hard copies for permit submittals and directs applicants to its online portal. A roofer that works in Stafford should already have an account there.

Contractor registration is on the list

The city's licenses and permits page names contractor registration as one of its construction permits. Ask whether the roofing company is registered with Stafford before you sign anything.

Six months to start

A Stafford permit expires if work does not begin within six months of issue or stops for longer than six months. Ask for the permit number and the date it was issued.

Which code edition

The city's building codes page lists the 2015 International Codes, including the residential code, adopted by Ordinance 1110 in 2018. Ask Development Services at 281-261-3940 whether a newer edition now applies to your job.

Look up a roofer's Stafford jobs in the city's own reports

Here is something most Houston suburbs do not offer. Stafford posts monthly building permit reports in its online archive, and they list each permit's date, type, class of work and contractor. Residential and commercial reroofs appear in them by name. Before you hire, you can search a few months of those reports for the company you are talking to and see whether it has actually pulled reroof permits in Stafford.

  1. Get the exact company name. Ask the roofer for the legal name it uses on permits, which may differ from its truck signs.
  2. Open the city's permit reports. They sit in the archive section of staffordtx.gov, one report per month.
  3. Search for reroof entries. Look for the company name next to a reroof class of work. A few entries in the last year is a good sign of local experience.
  4. Ask about anything missing. No entries does not mean a bad roofer, since it may work mainly in neighboring cities, but it is a fair question to raise.
  5. Check the permit for your job. Once the work is filed, your own reroof should appear in a later report. Keep the permit number with your house papers.

July 8, 2024Hurricane BerylKHOU reported that Fort Bend County crews had already collected about 150,000 cubic yards of storm debris in the two weeks after landfall.

Wind is the bigger threat to an older Stafford roof. A gust that breaks the seal strip under a row of shingles can leave them looking flat from the street while letting rain in the next time it blows sideways. Hail of an inch and a half can bruise shingles and crack vent caps without leaving a hole. If your roof rode out any of these storms and nobody has looked at it since, many roofers inspect at no charge; confirm that on the call.

What a Stafford roof estimate should spell out

HAR's neighborhood figures for the 77477 ZIP code, which covers Stafford, show average build years around the early 1970s for sections such as The Meadows and Stafford Oaks and the mid 1990s for Stafford Run. A house that age may carry more than one layer of shingles, older plywood or plank decking and vents that were never sized for today's attic rules. A clear estimate takes all of that into account in writing.

  • Complete tear-off down to the deck, with the number of existing layers noted.
  • A price per sheet for any decking that has to be replaced, so a surprise does not become a dispute.
  • Named underlayment, drip edge, starter strip and flashing, not just "per code".
  • Attic ventilation: what is there now and what will change.
  • The Stafford permit, filed in the roofer's name, with the number given to you.
  • The shingle maker's warranty registration and the roofer's own workmanship warranty, both in writing.

Some Stafford subdivisions have deed restrictions that control roof color. If yours does, Texas Property Code section 202.011 bars the association from prohibiting impact-resistant or energy-efficient shingles that look like the ones it already allows.
