Pearland roofing starts with one question: which county?

Roofing in Pearland, TX comes with a twist most homeowners never hear about until a bid arrives. The city covers parts of three counties, and only Brazoria County, where most of Pearland sits, is inside the Texas Department of Insurance coastal catastrophe area. That single line on the map decides whether a reroof needs a state windstorm inspection.

Pearland has grown fast. It incorporated in November 1959 with fewer than 2,000 people and counted 125,828 residents at the 2020 census, which makes it the largest city in Brazoria County. Much of that growth came in master planned communities west of State Highway 288 that are now reaching their first or second replacement roofs, often at the same time as their neighbors.

Houston Expert Roofers is a free phone line, not a roofer. Day or night, a recorded voice handles the first minute: it notes a quick description of the roof and your part of Pearland, and passes the call to an independent, locally owned roofing company that works here. That company does every part of the job and bills you for it.

Three counties, two sets of roofing rules

Find your county on your appraisal district record or tax statement, then read across. The city permit and the state windstorm program are separate things, and some Pearland addresses need both.

In the windstorm area, the Texas Department of Insurance explains that a WPI-8 is issued for work inspected while it is under way, by an appointed Texas licensed engineer or a TDI inspector. Work that was never inspected during construction can only be certified afterward as completed work (a WPI-8-E) by a licensed engineer, so the timing of the inspection matters. TDI's Angleton field office handles Brazoria County.

What the City of Pearland asks of a roofer

Pearland's Community Development department lists repairs to an existing structure among the work that needs a permit, and its contractor rules are specific. Contractors must be registered and have a contractor account with the city to apply for permits or schedule inspections. Their certificate of insurance must show at least $300,000 in general liability coverage and name the City of Pearland as the certificate holder.

That gives you a quick test. A roofing company already working in Pearland will have that account; a crew that arrived after the last storm may not. Ask before you sign, and ask the company to send its own certificate of insurance to you as well, since the city's copy protects the city, not your house.

The city's adopted codes page lists the 2021 International Residential Code and gives an ultimate design wind speed of 150 mph for wind loads; ask the permit office which wind speed applies to your job. Applications, payments and inspection requests run through the city's Cityworks permit portal, and the city warns that it only collects permit fees in person at the City Hall annex or through that portal. Permit questions go to the city's permit office at 281-652-1638.

Storms that tested Pearland roofs

Harvey, 2017: a water story

A City of Pearland presentation reported structural flooding in more than 1,700 residences, much of it along Clear Creek. That was rising water, not roofs giving way, and it is covered by flood insurance, not a homeowners policy. It is still a reason to keep roof and flood paperwork apart after a storm that brings both.

Beryl, July 2024: a wind story

Community Impact reported local stations measuring winds above 70 mph, and an assistant police chief called it the worst damage since Hurricane Ike in 2008. Structural damage was limited, but trees and power took a beating, and the city council waived building permit fees for storm repairs.

Hail, spring 2026: a fresh one

StormerSite, which compiles spotter and public reports, lists hail of an inch or more in and near Pearland in the storm of April 30 into May 1, 2026. Hail that size can bruise shingles without leaving a visible hole.

Most of Pearland's power lines belong to CenterPoint Energy, but Texas-New Mexico Power lists a small portion of Pearland in its Gulf Coast service area, so check your electric bill to see which utility to call about a downed line.

Why Pearland homeowners call the line

The windstorm step needs a local hand

On the Brazoria side, the inspection has to happen while the roof is open. Ask the company you reach how it schedules that inspection; a roofer that works here regularly will have a ready answer.

A registered company, not a passing crew

Pearland requires a contractor account for permits. A single call reaches a locally owned company, and you can confirm its city registration before anyone starts tearing shingles off.

Neighborhoods coming due together

Shadow Creek Ranch began selling homes in 2001, and Silverlake opened in 1995. When half a street needs roofs at once, an estimate based on your own roof is worth more than a door hanger.

No charge, any hour

Calling is free. If the roofer cannot answer right away, it receives your name, number, neighborhood and how urgent things are, and calls back. The company may pay the line a referral fee.

Shadow Creek Ranch, Silverlake and the HOA approval

Shadow Creek Ranch is the largest planned community in Pearland, about 3,500 acres west of State Highway 288 and south of Beltway 8; the city annexed the land in the late 1990s and home sales began in 2001. Because it straddles the Brazoria and Fort Bend County line, two neighbors can get bids that differ only because one needs a windstorm inspection. Silverlake, opened in 1995 with roughly 2,500 houses, sits just outside the city's western limits in unincorporated Brazoria County.

Both communities, like most Pearland subdivisions, have associations that approve roof color and style. Get the current guidelines before you collect bids. Texas Property Code section 202.011 stops an association from prohibiting impact-resistant or energy-efficient shingles that look like ones it already allows, which matters if hail makes a Class 4 shingle attractive. Ask your insurer whether such a roof changes your premium.

Questions to ask a Pearland roofing company

  • Is your company registered with the City of Pearland, with a contractor account in your name?
  • Which county is my house in, and does my job need a TDI windstorm inspection?
  • Who schedules that inspection, at what stage of the job, and when will I receive the WPI-8?
  • Which shingle, underlayment and fasteners will you use, and do they meet the windstorm requirements here?
  • What is your written price per sheet for damaged decking?
  • Who submits the HOA request, and which approved shingle does this estimate use?
