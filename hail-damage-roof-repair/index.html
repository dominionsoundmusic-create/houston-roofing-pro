Hail damage is not always visible from the ground

This is the single most important thing to understand about hail, and it is why so many Houston roofs go unrepaired until they leak.

Functional hail damage often looks like nothing. A hailstone does not usually punch a hole. It bruises the shingle — fracturing the mat underneath and knocking loose the mineral granules that protect it from the sun. From the driveway the roof looks fine. From four feet away on the roof itself, the bruises are obvious.

The damage shows up later, as the exposed asphalt degrades in Houston heat and the shingle begins to fail in the spots that were struck.

If your neighborhood had a hailstorm and your neighbors are having roofs replaced, get yours looked at even if it looks untouched. Insurance policies have deadlines, and a roof that looks fine today can be a denied claim in two years.

What actually counts as damage

What is foundWhat it means
Granule loss in circular patchesClassic hail bruising. The shingle's protection is gone in that spot
Soft or spongy spots underfootThe mat beneath has fractured. Functional damage
Dents in soft metal — gutters, vents, flashingCorroborating evidence. Adjusters look here first because metal records the hit clearly
Granules in the gutters after a stormWorth investigating, though some granule loss is normal on an ageing roof
Cracked or split shinglesUsually age or wind rather than hail — but it still needs repair

Why the first place to look is your gutters, not your roof

Do not climb up. Walk the perimeter and look at the soft metal: gutters, downspouts, the aluminium fascia, roof vents, the air conditioner's condenser fins. Hail dents metal visibly and unmistakably.

If those are dented, the roof took the same hits. If they are clean, it is less likely the roof was struck hard enough to matter.

The clock you are on

Texas policies carry deadlines for reporting damage, and they are shorter than most people assume. The practical rule is report it as soon as you are aware of it, and treat a known storm date in your neighborhood as the start of the clock rather than the day a leak appears.

Delay is the most common reason a legitimate claim is denied. Not because the damage was not real, but because too much time passed to prove when it happened.

What an inspection should produce

  • Photographs of specific damage, not a general shot of the roof.
  • A test square — a marked ten-by-ten foot area with hits counted. Adjusters use this method, and a contractor who knows it is speaking the adjuster's language.
  • The storm date, checked against weather records for your address.
  • A written report you keep, whether or not you file.

Repair or full replacement

Hail rarely damages one slope. It comes in at an angle, so the side facing the storm takes nearly all of it — but insurers and manufacturers often will not accept a partial replacement, because new shingles will not match weathered ones and a patched slope voids warranty coverage.
