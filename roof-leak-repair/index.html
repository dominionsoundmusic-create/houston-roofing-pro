The leak is almost never above the stain

Water does not fall straight down through a roof. It enters at one point, runs along the underside of the decking, travels down a rafter, and drips where something interrupts it. By the time it reaches your ceiling it can be ten or fifteen feet from where it got in, and usually uphill.

That is why chasing a leak from inside rarely works, and why a contractor who goes straight to the stain and starts replacing shingles above it is guessing.

Where leaks actually start

LocationWhy it leaks
Flashing around chimneys, walls and skylightsThe most common source by a wide margin. Metal and roofing expand at different rates and the seal eventually fails
Pipe boots — the rubber collars around plumbing ventsRubber cracks in Houston sun, often at ten to fifteen years while the roof itself is still good
Valleys where two slopes meetAll the water on both slopes funnels through here. Debris dams it and forces it sideways under the shingles
Nail popsA nail backs out and lifts the shingle above it. Small hole, steady drip
Ridge and hip capsExposed to the most wind, first to lift
Wind-lifted shinglesThe shingle looks fine lying flat but its seal is broken, so wind-driven rain gets underneath

Pipe boots are worth a specific mention. They are the single most common cause of a leak on an otherwise healthy roof, they cost very little to replace, and a contractor who wants to replace your whole roof because of one cracked boot is selling you something you do not need.

What to do while you wait

  1. Put a bucket under it and move anything that can be damaged.
  2. If the ceiling is bulging, pierce it. A small hole at the lowest point of the bulge releases the water in a controlled way. Left alone, the whole section comes down at once, which is a far bigger repair.
  3. Photograph everything — stains, the bulge, anything damaged. If this becomes an insurance claim you will want it.
  4. Do not go on the roof in the rain. Wet shingles are dangerous and you will not find anything in a storm anyway.

Why leaks are worse in Houston than the rainfall suggests

It is the combination. Heavy rain arrives sideways in a storm, which pushes water under shingles that would shed a vertical downpour perfectly well. Then the humidity means the attic never dries out properly between events, so a small leak grows mould and rots decking far faster than the same leak would in a dry climate.

A leak that seems minor is doing more damage here than it would elsewhere. The stain on the ceiling is the last thing to appear, not the first thing to happen.

What a proper leak repair involves

  • Finding the actual entry point, which usually means going into the attic and tracing the water back uphill along the rafters.
  • Checking the decking for rot. Wet decking must be replaced, not shingled over.
  • Repairing the cause — new flashing, new boot, resealed valley — rather than sealing the symptom with roofing cement.
  • Checking the rest of the roof for the same failure. If one pipe boot has cracked, the others are the same age.
