Pearland sits in a coastal windstorm county

Pearland does not feel coastal. It is thirty-some miles from the Gulf, surrounded by subdivisions and shopping centers. But most of Pearland is in Brazoria County, and Brazoria County is one of the fourteen first-tier coastal counties the Texas Department of Insurance designates as a catastrophe area. The city's own insurance filings note that its Brazoria County property sits in that zone.

In those counties, the Texas Windstorm Insurance Association is the insurer of last resort for wind and hail. To stay eligible for it, a re-roof has to be built to the windstorm code and inspected by a TDI-appointed inspector or engineer, who then issues a certificate of compliance, known as a WPI-8. Skip it, and the house may not qualify for TWIA coverage later, which can matter a great deal when you sell or when private insurers pull back after a hurricane.

Not all of Pearland is in Brazoria County. The city spills into Harris and Fort Bend counties, and those parts are outside the catastrophe area. Check your appraisal record for the county, then ask your roofer whether the job needs a windstorm inspection.

What to ask a Pearland roofer about windstorm

  • Will you get the WPI-8? The roofer should schedule the TDI inspection and hand you the certificate at the end.
  • Which products are you using? Windstorm-rated work uses shingles, underlayment and fasteners installed to the tested method. Ask for the product names in the contract.
  • When is the inspection? Some checks happen during installation, before the underlayment and nailing are covered. A roofer who says it can all be done at the end may not have done this before.

City permits and registration

Pearland runs its permits through the city's Community Development Department, and its permit guidance asks for a contractor registered with the city, with liability insurance on file. That is a useful filter in itself. A roofing company that already works in Pearland will be registered; one that arrived after the last storm may not be.

Hurricanes and the Pearland roof

Pearland took some of Hurricane Harvey's heaviest rain in 2017 and was hit hard again by Hurricane Beryl's wind in July 2024. Wind is the bigger enemy of a roof. It lifts shingle tabs, breaks the seal strips underneath, and leaves a roof that looks fine from the street but lets water in during the next blow. The master-planned neighborhoods that filled Pearland in the 2000s, such as Shadow Creek Ranch and Silverlake, are reaching the age when those first roofs come due.

Insurance and the deductible

You file the claim. A roofer can document the damage and meet the adjuster, but Texas law bars a contractor from acting as your public adjuster, and it requires you to pay your own deductible. Any offer to waive or absorb it is illegal. See storm damage, hail insurance claims and the free claim checklist.

Common questions

Does a new roof in Pearland need a windstorm inspection?

In the Brazoria County part of Pearland, a re-roof needs a Texas Department of Insurance windstorm inspection and WPI-8 certificate to keep the home eligible for TWIA wind and hail coverage. The Harris and Fort Bend County parts of Pearland are outside the designated catastrophe area.

What is a WPI-8?

It is the certificate of compliance TDI issues after an appointed inspector or engineer confirms the work meets the windstorm building standards. TWIA uses it to decide whether a home is insurable.

Does my roofer need to be registered in Pearland?

Pearland's permit guidance calls for a contractor registered with the city, with liability insurance on file. Ask to see the registration.

Are roofers licensed in Texas?

No. Texas has no state roofing license, so registration, insurance and local references are what you have to go on.

Which areas do you take calls from?
