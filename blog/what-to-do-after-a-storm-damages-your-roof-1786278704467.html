What to Do After a Storm Damages Your Roof: A Houston Homeowner's Guide

Houston's weather is no joke. From powerful thunderstorms and heavy hail to hurricane-force winds and torrential rain, our city takes a beating year after year. When a major storm rolls through, your roof is the first line of defense for your home — and sometimes, it pays the price. If you suspect your roof has been damaged, knowing exactly what to do in the hours and days that follow can save you thousands of dollars and prevent serious secondary damage like mold, leaks, and structural issues.

This practical guide walks Houston homeowners through every step to take after storm damage hits your roof, from immediate safety checks to working with your insurance company and finding a trustworthy local roofing contractor.

1. Stay Safe First — Don't Rush Outside

In the immediate aftermath of a storm, your instinct might be to run outside and check the damage. Resist that urge until it's completely safe to do so. Downed power lines, standing water, sharp debris, and unstable structures can all pose serious risks.

Once the storm has fully passed and you've confirmed there are no immediate hazards, do a careful walk around the exterior of your home from a safe distance. Look for obvious signs of roof damage such as missing shingles, large debris on the roof, dented gutters, or visible holes. Do not climb onto the roof yourself — wet or damaged roofing materials are extremely slippery and structurally unpredictable. Leave the up-close inspection to a professional Houston roofing contractor.

2. Check for Interior Damage and Stop Active Leaks

Head inside and inspect your ceilings, attic, and upper walls for signs of water intrusion. Look for water stains, bubbling paint, sagging drywall, or dripping water. If you have attic access, grab a flashlight and look for daylight coming through the roof deck or wet insulation.

If you find an active leak, take these immediate steps to minimize damage:

  • Place buckets or towels to catch dripping water
  • Move furniture, electronics, and valuables away from the affected area
  • If water is pooling near electrical fixtures, turn off electricity to that area at the breaker box
  • Take photos and videos of all interior damage — you'll need this for your insurance claim

Temporary fixes like placing a tarp over the damaged area can help prevent further water damage until a professional can make permanent repairs. Many Houston roofing companies, including Houston Roofing Pro, offer emergency tarping services after major storm events.

3. Document Everything for Your Insurance Claim

Before any cleanup or repairs begin, thorough documentation is absolutely critical. Insurance companies require solid evidence of storm damage to process your claim, and the more detailed your records, the smoother the process will go.

Here's what to document:

  • Photos and videos of all visible exterior damage, including missing shingles, dented gutters, damaged flashing, and any debris on the roof
  • Interior damage including water stains, leaks, and damaged belongings
  • Date and time stamps on all media files
  • Written notes describing the storm and the damage you observed
  • Weather reports or news articles confirming the storm event in the Houston area

Store all of this in a cloud folder or email it to yourself so nothing gets lost. This documentation package is your most powerful tool when dealing with your insurance adjuster.

4. Contact Your Homeowner's Insurance Company

After documenting the damage, call your insurance company as soon as possible to file a storm damage claim. Most policies have a window of time in which you must report damage, and waiting too long can jeopardize your claim.

When you call, have your policy number ready and be prepared to describe the damage in detail. Ask your insurer about:

  • Your deductible amount (Texas has a separate wind and hail deductible on many policies)
  • The timeline for sending out an adjuster
  • Whether temporary repairs are covered
  • What documentation they need from you

Once the adjuster visits, having a trusted local roofing contractor present can be extremely beneficial. An experienced Houston roofer knows what adjusters look for and can advocate on your behalf to ensure all legitimate damage is included in your claim — not just what's easy to spot from the ground.

5. Get a Professional Roof Inspection from a Local Houston Roofer

This step is one of the most important — and the one most homeowners either delay or skip entirely. Storm damage isn't always visible from the ground. Hail can bruise asphalt shingles in ways that look minor but dramatically shorten your roof's lifespan. Wind can lift shingles and compromise the adhesive seal without completely removing them. Even small punctures or cracks in flashing can allow water to seep in over weeks or months before a visible leak appears inside your home.

A certified roofing professional will inspect:

  • Shingles for hail impacts, cracks, curling, and missing granules
  • Flashing around chimneys, vents, and skylights
  • Gutters and downspouts for dents and debris buildup
  • The roof deck for soft spots or moisture damage
  • Ridge caps and ventilation components

6. Watch Out for Storm Chasers and Roofing Scams

After every major storm in Houston, out-of-town contractors flood the area looking to take advantage of desperate homeowners. These so-called "storm chasers" often offer suspiciously low prices, demand large upfront deposits, and disappear before the job is finished — or do shoddy work that fails within a year.

Protect yourself by following these guidelines:

  • Only hire locally established roofing companies with a verifiable Houston-area address and history
  • Check for a valid Texas contractor license and proper insurance (general liability and workers' comp)
  • Read Google reviews and look for long-term customer feedback
  • Get multiple written estimates before committing
  • Never pay more than 10–15% upfront — reputable contractors don't require large deposits
  • Be wary of anyone who offers to "waive your deductible" — this is insurance fraud in Texas

Choosing a trusted, local Houston roofing contractor protects both your home and your insurance claim.

Conclusion: Act Quickly, But Act Smart

Storm damage to your roof can feel overwhelming, but taking the right steps quickly and methodically will protect your home, your finances, and your peace of mind. Prioritize safety, document everything, contact your insurance company promptly, and get a professional inspection from a roofing contractor you can trust. The longer storm damage goes unaddressed in Houston's humid climate, the more expensive and complicated the repairs become.
