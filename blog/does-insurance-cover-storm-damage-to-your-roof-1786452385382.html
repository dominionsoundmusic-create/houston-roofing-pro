Does Insurance Cover Storm Damage to Your Roof? A Houston Homeowner's Guide

If you've just weathered a Houston thunderstorm, hailstorm, or hurricane and you're staring up at your roof wondering what happened — you're not alone. Storm damage is one of the most common roofing issues homeowners in the Greater Houston area face, and the first question most people ask is: will my homeowners insurance pay for this?

The short answer is: it depends. But the longer answer is far more useful — and that's exactly what we're going to break down for you. From understanding your policy to filing a claim the right way, this guide will help you navigate the process with confidence.

What Types of Storm Damage Are Typically Covered?

Most standard homeowners insurance policies in Texas cover what's called "sudden and accidental" damage caused by specific weather events. For Houston homeowners, this commonly includes:

  • Hail damage — Hailstorms can crack, dent, or strip the granules off asphalt shingles, leaving your roof vulnerable to leaks.
  • Wind damage — High winds from tropical storms or severe thunderstorms can tear off shingles, lift flashing, or even cause structural damage.
  • Hurricane damage — Falling debris, sustained wind, and heavy rain can cause significant and widespread roof damage.
  • Lightning strikes — A direct strike can cause fire damage or structural compromise to your roofing system.
  • Fallen trees or branches — If a storm causes a tree to fall on your roof, that impact damage is generally covered.

What's typically not covered is damage caused by neglect, normal wear and tear, or pre-existing conditions. If your roof was already in poor shape before the storm hit, your insurer may only partially cover the damage — or deny the claim altogether.

Understanding Your Houston Homeowners Insurance Policy

Before you file a claim, it's important to understand the key terms in your policy. Here's what to look for:

ACV vs. RCV Coverage
Your policy may pay out based on either Actual Cash Value (ACV) or Replacement Cost Value (RCV). ACV factors in depreciation — meaning you'll get less money based on the age of your roof. RCV pays the full cost to replace your roof with a similar one at today's prices. RCV coverage is more expensive, but far better for significant storm damage claims.

Your Deductible
Many Texas policies now include a separate wind and hail deductible, which is often calculated as a percentage of your home's insured value — not a flat dollar amount. On a $300,000 home with a 2% deductible, that's $6,000 out of pocket before insurance kicks in. Know your numbers before you file.

Exclusions and Limitations
Read the fine print. Some policies exclude certain roofing materials or limit payouts based on the age of the roof. If your roof is over 20 years old, some insurers may only offer ACV coverage regardless of your plan.

How to Document Storm Damage to Your Roof

Proper documentation is everything when it comes to a successful insurance claim. Here's what Houston homeowners should do immediately after a storm:

  1. Stay safe first. Don't climb onto your roof, especially in wet or unstable conditions. Leave the inspection to professionals.
  2. Take photos and videos. Document visible damage from the ground — missing shingles, dents in gutters, damaged vents, fallen debris. Also photograph any interior damage like ceiling stains or water intrusion.
  3. Note the date and storm details. Save local weather reports or news coverage of the storm. This helps confirm the "sudden event" requirement insurers look for.
  4. Prevent further damage. Most policies require you to take reasonable steps to prevent additional damage. Cover exposed areas with tarps or boards if it's safe to do so. Keep your receipts — those costs may be reimbursable.
  5. Get a professional roof inspection. A licensed Houston roofing contractor can provide a detailed written assessment of the storm damage, which is invaluable when working with your insurance adjuster.

The Insurance Claims Process: Step by Step

Filing a storm damage claim in Texas doesn't have to be overwhelming. Here's a simple breakdown of what to expect:

Step 1: Contact Your Insurance Company
Call your insurer as soon as possible to report the damage. Most companies have 24/7 claims lines and mobile apps. Texas law requires insurers to acknowledge your claim within 15 days of filing.

Step 2: Schedule an Adjuster Inspection
Your insurance company will send an adjuster to assess the damage. We strongly recommend having a trusted Houston roofing contractor present during this inspection. A knowledgeable roofer can point out storm-related damage that adjusters may miss and ensure nothing is overlooked.

Step 3: Review the Estimate
The adjuster will provide a damage estimate and payout amount. If you believe it's too low or incomplete, you have the right to dispute it. A second opinion from your roofing contractor can help support your case.

Step 4: Choose Your Contractor and Begin Repairs
Once your claim is approved, work with a reputable local roofing company to complete the repairs. Be cautious of out-of-town storm chasers who show up door-to-door after major weather events — they often do subpar work and disappear when problems arise.

Red Flags to Watch Out For

Unfortunately, storm season in Houston also brings out unscrupulous contractors looking to take advantage of stressed homeowners. Here's how to protect yourself:

  • Avoid anyone who guarantees to waive your deductible. This is insurance fraud in Texas and can void your coverage entirely.
  • Don't sign anything before your claim is approved. Some contractors use "Assignment of Benefits" agreements that sign over your insurance rights — this can lead to disputes and delays.
  • Verify licensing and insurance. All legitimate Texas roofing contractors should be able to provide proof of general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage.
  • Get everything in writing. A detailed written estimate and contract protects both you and the contractor.

What If My Claim Is Denied?

A denied claim isn't necessarily the end of the road. Texas homeowners have options:

First, request a written explanation of the denial. Then, gather additional documentation — photos, weather records, and a professional roofing inspection report — to support an appeal. You can also request an independent appraisal under most Texas policies, or consult with a public adjuster who works on your behalf (not the insurance company's). If all else fails, the Texas Department of Insurance can assist with complaints and disputes.
